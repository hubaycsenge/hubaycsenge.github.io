The central claim as it now stands after ingesting 18 sources. Steps 1–4 are well supported; step 5 is partly settled by prior work; the contribution has moved to steps 6–8.

The argument in steps

  1. Robots in social roles need to handle affect. Recognising a partner's emotional state and returning appropriate feedback improves cooperation and acceptance research-proposal — qualified: it helps when the expressed emotion is correctly read, and can backfire when it is not (emotion-attribution).
  2. People attribute emotions to artificial agents, spontaneously and accurately, and act on the attribution. Strongly evidenced (gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution, lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution).
  3. But robots should not imitate humans. function-orientedness and interspecific-hri, motivated by the ethological reinterpretation of the uncanny-valley (miklosi-2017-ethorobotics).
  4. Dogs are the right model species — and the transfer claim is now properly stated: not imitation but functional analogy, dog behaviour as a source of legible primitives re-expressed in whatever embodiment is available (dog-human-interaction-model). Demonstrated across five robots spanning a bare holonomic base to an 11-DOF expressive head (lakatos-2014-embodiments-demo).
  5. The engine needs an evolutionarily grounded base model. Prior work settled on a hybrid: Panksepp for architecture, dog ethology for the state set (szabo-2011-emotional-engine). See emotion-model-comparison.
  6. The engine must fuse human affect with the robot's own internal state. No prior model does this. emotion-engine
  7. Behaviour is learnt, not hand-authored — reinforcement learning over an ethologically-derived action set, with behaviour-trees (py_trees_ros) executing the primitives. A double departure from the lineage, which used fuzzy automata and state-space models and authored every rule by hand. Justified by social-competence's own requirement of "responsiveness to learning and training". See rl-for-behaviour-learning.
  8. The contribution is real-time, autonomous operation in real environments, validated on human subjects (validation-study).

Where the contribution actually is, after ingest

Sharpened claim

"Real-time event detection in real environments" is defensible but vague. The corpus supports something more precise and harder to dispute:

1. Nobody has closed the perception-to-emotion loop — and this is now known to be field-wide, not lineage-specific. Every emotional engine in this lineage runs on hand-labelled events from a GUI (szabo-2011-emotional-engine); every human study of dog-derived emotional behaviour was wizard-of-oz (lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution, gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution).

belle-2019-programming-with-affect closes the argument. It is an entirely independent tradition — game AI, Barbados and Kingston, OCC and PAD rather than Panksepp and dog ethology — and it stops in exactly the same place: every emotion is fired by a scripted dialogue choice with a hard-wired trigger. Two traditions, no contact between them, same architecture, same missing link.

This thesis builds the thing that was faked.

2. Nobody models the robot's own state. Fusing battery, motor and sensor health with social perception is unattempted across the entire corpus, and is what makes the robot's emotion its own rather than a mirror of the human's.

3. Nobody has done light-and-motion-only emotional expression. Every measured robot has a face or articulated appendages (emotion-recognition-benchmarks). robot-platform has neither. There is no baseline; establishing one is a contribution.

4. Nobody's robot in this tradition learns. Every model in the corpus is fixed at design time, while miklosi-2017-ethorobotics lists "responsiveness to learning and training" as a requirement of social-competence. The gap between the framework's stated requirement and every implementation of it is a contribution in itself. See rl-for-behaviour-learning.

Where the argument is still weakest

  • Step 6 is unspecified. Internal state and perceived human affect are in incompatible units, and no source offers a fusion scheme. This is the contribution and the least developed part. Q2.
  • Step 8 has no operational definition. No latency budget, no criterion, no named environment. Q3. The one number that would partly settle it — end-to-end lidar → detection → tick → response latency — is cheaply measurable on the existing stack.
  • The perception stream may not exist. No camera or microphone is documented on robot-platform. If confirmed, multimodal-emotion-recognition must be rebuilt around proxemic and kinematic cues — plausible, arguably better-aligned, but a different chapter. Confirm before designing.
  • Step 7 is now supported, and partly pre-empted. colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees supplies the behaviour-tree theory, the RL-BT integration and a safety mechanism. belle-2019-programming-with-affect supplies the emotion half — and it gets there first: BT nodes that make affective state first-class in action selection are prior art as of 2019. The thesis cannot claim that idea. What it can claim is everything EMoBeT lacks — a robot, sensing, learning, internal state, a real environment, and any evaluation at all (their §V is worked traces; the authors defer viability testing to future work). The reward signal remains undefined — Q14.
  • sutton-barto-2018-rl undercuts step 8 rather than supporting it. Its §17.5 states that current methods "struggle to learn rapidly in the incremental, online settings" and names safe embedding of RL agents in physical environments as an open problem. That makes real-time real-environment learning a defensible target but not a solved engineering step.
  • The evidence base is one research network. Every empirical result supporting the dog model comes from people who proposed it, including the papers that look Japanese (hungarian-ethorobotics-network). Independent or critical literature is the most valuable thing missing.
  • Nothing tests social-competence as the framework defines it — it emerges over time, and every study in the corpus is single-session.

Added 2026-07-28, from ortony-2022-cognitive-structure

  • There is an unnamed stage between steps 1 and 5. The argument runs from events matter to the engine needs a base model, with no account of how an event becomes an appraised event. Every implementation in the corpus hand-labelled it. That stage has a name — appraisal — a formal model (occ-model), and a literature the wiki has only just opened. New Q17; see appraisal-theory.
  • Step 2's mechanism is now sourced, and it favours the platform. Attribution turns on the object being construed as emotion-capable, and the criterion OCC gives is responsiveness and apparent reciprocity — gaze exchange, contingent reaction to small movements — not resemblance. Dogs are its paradigm case and domestic robots are named explicitly (p. 180). This strengthens steps 2, 3 and 4 simultaneously, from a source outside the network flagged above. See emotion-attribution.
  • Step 8 may be aimed at the wrong target. Gratch and Marsella argue surface expressions have "only a loose connection to internal states" and that affect inference becomes accurate only with knowledge of preceding events and the person's goals (p. 242). If so, real-time recognition from pose is insufficient in principle, not merely in practice. New Q19; see multimodal-emotion-recognition.
  • Step 7 now departs from unanimous practice. OCC recommends hand-coding cognition–emotion relationships over learning them (p. 219) — a third independent vote alongside Szabó and Belle et al., with no source in the corpus voting for learning. The departure is defensible and narrower than it looks (they hand-code appraisal; this thesis learns policy), but it has to be argued explicitly. See rl-for-behaviour-learning.
  • A premise the thesis has never stated is now contested in the corpus. OCC's authors deny that a robot can have emotions at all (p. 219). The objection dissolves under functional language, which plutchik-model supplies and function-orientedness already implies — but the thesis should say so in the introduction rather than leave it to a viva. machine-emotion-question, Q18.
