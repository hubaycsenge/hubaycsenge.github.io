Two RO-MAN 2012 papers from Chuo University (Niitsuma, Hashimoto) with Korondi and Gácsi as Hungarian co-authors. Not an independent corroboration — this is the same collaboration network extended to Japan (hungarian-ethorobotics-network) — but it is the only application-domain work in the corpus.

Shared setting

Intelligent Space (iSpace): an environment instrumented with distributed intelligent network devices (DINDs = sensors + processors + network devices), able to track multiple people simultaneously in different locations. A mobile robot serves as the physical agent that delivers information wherever the caregiver happens to be.

Application: elderly monitoring / home-care support. The robot must inform a caregiver of events without disrupting their primary activity. This is a concrete answer to what a socially competent robot is actually for — the functional niche that function-orientedness demands but research-proposal never specifies.

Ichikawa et al. — behavioural factors

Extends the existing dog-based robot behaviour model by adding behavioural factors that give the same behaviour different characteristics (a personality/ temperament layer). Subjects evaluated their impressions of the robot under different factor settings, in a simulator.

Result: subjects formed the impressions the authors expected from the factors chosen, without any prior knowledge about the robot. Conclusion drawn: factors can be tuned to a caregiver's preference.

Numakunai et al. — exploratory behaviour

Adds an exploratory behaviour model, taking dogs' exploration in the Strange Situation Test (attachment-sst) as the base. Contribution: a map-building method that records not just the positions of people and the robot but also the robot's movement history and willingness to explore, so a target and path can be selected according to behavioural characteristics.

Result: subjects perceived different impressions from the different exploratory behaviours.

Relevance

  • Behavioural factors are a temperament layer — the same role played by Szabó's dropped long-term layer (szabo-2010-emotional-engine) and, in animals, by individual variation. Two independent routes to the same need.
  • Exploration is already modelled as a state in kovacs-fri-automaton-series (ExplorationLevel). Numakunai turns it into actual navigation. For a mecanum-drive robot with no expressive face, how it moves is a primary expressive channel — see robot-platform.
  • Home care gives a defensible answer to "what real environment?" (Q3).

Limitations

  • Both evaluated in simulation, not with a live robot. Ichikawa explicitly notes the subjects' viewpoint differed from a live experiment and defers that to future work.
  • Impression evaluation only — no task performance, no acceptance measure, no emotion recognition.
  • Neither paper models emotion; they model behaviour characteristics.
  • Extraction of these two PDFs was partial; the experimental details (N, measures, statistics) were not fully recovered and should be checked in the originals before citing numbers. #needs-source
