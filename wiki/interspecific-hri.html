Human–robot interaction should be represented as an inter-specific relationship rather than an intra-specific one — an interaction between two different kinds, "characterised by not only different anatomy but also distinct communicational abilities" miklosi-2017-ethorobotics, gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution.

Originally proposed in Miklósi & Gácsi (2012), On the utilization of social animals as a model for social robotics — cited by nearly every source in the corpus and not itself held. See citation-backlog.

Why the analogy is apt

lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution gives the clearest statement of what human–animal interaction shares with human–robot interaction, and human–human interaction does not:

Property Human–human Human–animal / human–robot
Symmetry symmetric asymmetric
Onset develops from birth may start at any age
Language based on language non-verbal on one side
Complexity very complex much simpler

Because human–human interaction is symmetric, lifelong and language-based, robot designers mimicking it will never produce a convincing "human" — and the mismatch between appearance and behaviour violates user expectations, producing unease. That is the uncanny-valley restated as a design failure.

Consequences

  • The robot is measured by whether the interaction works, not by how human it seems. Aligns with function-orientedness.
  • Cross-species social competence becomes the relevant precedent, which is what justifies dog-human-interaction-model.
  • Evaluation should target acceptance and cooperation, not likeness — see validation-study.
  • Partnership is an outcome of interaction, not a property of the robot (social-competence). Robots are "constructed for a social process and not for a social state".

What the framework still does not say

It does not specify how emotion should work across the species boundary — which is precisely this thesis' territory. The framework licenses an affective layer via emotion-attribution but supplies no model; the model comes separately from panksepp-affective-systems via szabo-2011-emotional-engine. The join between the ethological framing and the affective architecture is made by analogy, not derived. Worth noting as a soft spot in thesis.
