Live uncertainties, each to be closed by a source, an experiment, or a recorded decision. Q1 and Q7 are largely answered, Q6 has a template. Q11 is answered by Csenge's decision to use RL for the behaviour layer and Q9 is partly dissolved by it; Q14–Q16 open in their place.

Q1 — Which emotion model grounds the engine? (recommendation filed, awaiting sign-off)

Prior work in the lineage settled on a hybrid: Panksepp for architecture, dog ethology for the state set (szabo-2011-emotional-engine).

Update 2026-07-28: both primaries are now held — panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness, panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits and plutchik-1982-psychoevolutionary — and a recommendation is on emotion-model-comparison: Panksepp for architecture, Plutchik for the state set and behaviour mapping, on the grounds that Plutchik's functional language is function-orientedness in emotion-theory form and that his behavioural column is implementable on a faceless robot. It also explains surprise, which Szabó implemented and Panksepp has no circuit for.

Not filed as a decision. Q1 stays open until confirmed.

Update 2026-07-28, OCC held: ortony-2022-cognitive-structure is now in the corpus and the recommendation above is untouched (Csenge's steer: record and decide later). But the ingest suggests Q1 is the wrong shape. An engine needs an appraisal front end, a state set, and a behaviour mapping; the recommendation covers the last two and is silent on the first, which is exactly where szabo-2011-emotional-engine and belle-2019-programming-with-affect both fell back on hand-labelled events. occ-model is the only model in the corpus that specifies the event→state path, and it has no behavioural output at all. Proposal: split Q1 into Q1a (state set — recommendation stands) and Q1b (appraisal layer — untouched, see Q17). See emotion-model-comparison.

Two sub-questions are now answered: intensity and blending come natively from Plutchik's cone and dyads; dynamics ("emotions outlast the stimuli that activated them") are a sourced Panksepp attribute rather than an engineering convenience.

Q2 — How is the robot's internal state commensurable with perceived human affect? (open — but the data blocker is gone)

Battery, motor and sensor health versus human emotion estimates, in incompatible units. No source in the corpus attempts this — the gap is universal. Modulator, separate channel, or mapped into the same space? Unresolved. This is now the strongest candidate for the thesis' original contribution. See emotion-engine. #needs-source

Update 2026-07-28, theory: two independently derived models agree that internal state is a different category of affect from emotion. Panksepp splits homeostatic affects (interoceptive — hunger, thirst) from emotional ones (panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness); Plutchik distinguishes motivation (changing internal states, absence of homeostatically significant stimuli, rhythmic) from emotion (external stimuli, presence of a survival-relevant event) (plutchik-1982-psychoevolutionary).

That converges with reward-design's route from sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3: internal state as a modulator of what is rewarding, not as another dimension of the affect vector. Three sources, three traditions, one architecture — the strongest support Q2 has had.

Update 2026-07-28, platform: the telemetry exists and is richer than assumed. OpenCRState.msg carries wheel velocities, positions, currents, accelerations, per-wheel error states, DMS distance, battery voltage and 9-axis IMU; orin_battery_state adds INA219 power data (mecanumbot-repos). Per-wheel fault state is a genuine "how am I doing" signal, not just a charge level. What remains is purely the modelling question — reward-design offers internal state as a modulator of what is rewarding as the best-sourced route.

Update 2026-07-28, a fourth route: appraisal-theory offers a different way to make the two commensurable — not by converting units, but by deriving both from the same representation. Gratch and Marsella argue appraisal variables are read off the judgements any competent planner already makes: goal congruence, likelihood, control (ortony-2022-cognitive-structure pp. 235–237). If desirability is computed from py_trees_ros blackboard state, then per-wheel fault state and goal status are already in one representation, and commensurability is structural rather than constructed. Speculative — no source proposes this for internal state, and OCC has no interoceptive component at all. But it is the first route that would not require a conversion the wiki has been unable to specify. #needs-source

Q3 — What makes "real-time in a real environment" defensible?

Still needs an operational definition: end-to-end latency budget, what counts as a real environment, what failure looks like. Cheapest partial answer available: measure lidar scan → DR-SPAAM detection → py_trees tick → LED/motion response on robot-platform. The home-care setting of ichikawa-numakunai-2012-roman is the corpus' answer to "which environment".

Update 2026-07-28: colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees Ch. 9 supplies an analytic complement to the measured one. Stochastic BTs convert to Discrete Time Markov Chains, yielding Mean Time To Succeed, Mean Time To Fail and success probability over time, composable across subtrees. That gives a defensible answer to "will this complete in time, and how often will it fail" without running the robot — and it is stated in terms of the actual tree, not a generic claim. Pair it with the measured end-to-end latency. Note the platform is now heavier than when Q3 was written: two neural detectors plus fusion, and "checking all the conditions can be expensive" is a listed BT disadvantage.

Q4 — Does the dog model survive contact with implementation? (partly answered, and less severe)

lakatos-2014-embodiments-demo shows the same design working across four embodiments including one (Sunflower) whose expressive channels were an LED panel and motion — close to robot-platform. But no one has measured recognition rates on a light-and-motion-only robot.

Update 2026-07-28: the robot has a neck and two grabbers, so it is not light-and-motion-only. Available re-expressions now include head up and down dynamically (Gácsi's anger signal) alongside approach, back, turn away, orient and motionless. Tail and ear analogues are still missing. mecanumbot-behaviours already implements neck/gripper gesture as a distinct experimental condition, so this is partly measurable rather than merely arguable.

Q5 — Does the affective computing literature smuggle in anthropomorphism? (a clear datapoint)

Update 2026-07-28: belle-2019-programming-with-affect is the answer in miniature. The nearest prior art to this thesis' decision layer is built from OCC appraisal, a PAD mood space, the Five Factor Model of personality and scripted human dialogue — every component drawn from human psychology, applied to human-shaped characters having human conversations. Nothing in it would survive translation to a wheeled robot with no face.

That is not a criticism of the paper, which is about game NPCs. It is a demonstration that the BT-plus-emotion tradition the thesis inherits its architecture from carries anthropomorphic commitments in its content, and that function-orientedness has to be applied deliberately rather than assumed. Also worth noting their explicit design stance — simulate human-like emotion "regardless of whether it is an optimal outcome" — which is the opposite of what rl-for-behaviour-learning proposes.

Still live, and now sharper: if robot-platform has no camera, the facial-expression route is closed anyway, and the proxemic alternative is more ethologically consistent. The risk inverts — the temptation will be to add a camera to do things the human way.

Q6 — What is the evaluation instrument for the human study? (template found)

lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution and gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution provide a full protocol — NARS, open-ended-then-forced-choice, 4-point attribution scoring with κ = 0.84, Solomon Coder, non-parametric statistics, wizard-of-oz baseline. Collected in validation-study. Still missing: a cooperation or task-performance measure. #needs-source

Q7 — Which platform, and what does it constrain? (answered, and corrected)

Mecanumbot: ROS 2 Humble on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, OpenCR, LD08 lidar, CSI camera, DR-SPAAM + YOLO-pose person detection, py_trees_ros, four RGB LED panels, neck and two grabbers, mecanum drive. See robot-platform.

Corrected 2026-07-28. The two urgent follow-ups are both resolved: there is a camera, and internal-state telemetry is exposed. The earlier answer said Raspberry Pi, no camera and no telemetry — all wrong. One sensing question survives: is mecanumbot_audio capture, playback, or both? It decides whether vocal affect input and Morton's-rules vocalisation are on the table.

New questions from this ingest

Q8 — When does emotion expression help, and when does it backfire?

gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution cites Petisca et al. (2014) that robot emotion expression sometimes worsens human perception. lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution observed the mechanism: misread "fear" read as indifference, met with anger and embarrassment. Step 1 of thesis needs qualifying. Obtain Petisca et al. #needs-source

Q9 — Fuzzy automaton, behaviour tree, or both? (partly dissolved)

The lineage has two parallel architectures that were never integrated: fuzzy-automaton-fri for behaviour state, szabo-2011-emotional-engine for emotional state. Neither cites the other as a component. Meanwhile robot-platform ships py_trees, a third option. Integrating them is an opportunity; picking one is a decision that has to be made and justified. See behaviour-trees.

Update after the RL decision: the contest partly dissolves if rl-for-behaviour-learning is adopted — the behaviour tree becomes the executor of primitives and the learnt policy the selector, so they are not competing for the same job. What the fuzzy automaton uniquely supplied, a hand-authored model of behavioural sequencing, is what learning displaces. That loss is the residue of Q9 and is real.

Update 2026-07-28, largely resolved: colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees settles the architectural half. BTs generalise FSMs, hierarchical FSMs, Subsumption, Teleo-Reactive programs and Decision Trees, so nothing expressible in the automaton is lost by structure; and the BT does more than execute — §8.4 says it decomposes the RL problem into local state spaces. Measured against that, the FSM-based learner's node count grew exponentially while the BT's growth rate declined (§8.2.4.1).

Decision: behaviour tree, with learning nodes. One residue survives and should be stated in the thesis rather than buried: the automaton does behaviour fusion (continuous blending by necessity level — "somewhat anxious while still exploring") and a BT selects. Blending would need explicit parallel or decorator machinery. See behaviour-trees.

Q10 — Can automatic behaviour coding replace human coders here?

vincze-2021-automatic-coding codes behaviour elements from spatial position data alone, and robot-platform produces exactly that via lidar + leg detection. The authors name real-participant localisation as the blocker; this platform may remove it. Would cut the most expensive part of validation-study and give an independent measure.

Q11 — What is reinforcement learning actually for? (answered)

Answered by decision, 2026-07-28: RL is to teach robot-platform behaviour patterns. Which component: the behaviour policy, not the emotional engine. Which chapters: Ch. 14–15 and §17.4 of sutton-barto-2018-rl.

The trade-off with hand-authored rules is answered — not dismissed — by keeping the ethologically-derived primitives as the admissible action set A(s) (§14.3) and learning only the selection over them. The full argument, the layering, a first MDP formulation and its risks are in rl-for-behaviour-learning. Which reward signal remains open: Q14.

Q12 — Should fear really override everything?

emotion-arbitration gives fear absolute priority above medium intensity, on a triune-brain justification the authors themselves call obsolete. Fear is also the least reliably recognised emotion on every robot measured (~48%), and is misread in a way that provokes hostile user reactions. The rule maximises exposure to the known failure mode. See emotion-recognition-benchmarks.

Q13 — Should the input set be proxemic rather than affective? (reframed — the premise was false)

Asked on the assumption that robot-platform had no camera. It has one, and mecanumbot-perception already publishes 17-keypoint body pose fused with lidar into people in map space.

The question survives in better form: the input set is neither bare proxemics nor facial expression but bodily — posture, orientation, gesture, gait, spatial arrangement. That is closer to what a dog reads, keeps Q5 answered, and is strictly richer than the attachment-sst rule base needs. It is still affect inference from behaviour rather than emotion recognition, and the thesis still has to say so. See multimodal-emotion-recognition.

New questions from the Sutton & Barto ingest

Q14 — What collapses the emotional state into a scalar reward?

The reward hypothesis requires a single number (sutton-barto-2018-rl §3.2); the emotional state is a five-dimensional vector (szabo-2011-emotional-engine). Sutton & Barto concede that scalar reward is "at odds with" the qualitatively different appetitive and aversive systems of real animals, and explicitly pass over the problem (§14 terminology).

Weighted sum, valence projection, a separate reward per behaviour unit, or a vector-valued signal as §15.4 hints dopamine may actually be? This is the central unsolved design decision created by the RL commitment. See reward-design and rl-for-behaviour-learning. #needs-source

Q15 — Is the emotional engine the reward function, or a state feature?

Two readable architectures, predicting different behaviour. Either the engine computes R and the policy never sees the emotion vector — emotion is motivationally efficacious — or the emotion vector is part of the state and reward comes from elsewhere (task success, human response) — emotion is merely informative. Nothing in the corpus forces the choice. See emotion-engine.

Q16 — Should habit formation be a measured outcome of the validation study?

habitual-vs-goal-directed supplies a documented process by which repeated experience shifts behavioural control from goal-directed to habitual, and an instrument for detecting it (outcome devaluation, Adams & Dickinson 1981; Adams 1982). social-competence is defined as emerging over time and no study in the corpus tests it. This would turn "partnership forms" into a measurable claim — at the cost of a longitudinal design with no precedent in the corpus. See validation-study.

Q17 — What computes the appraisal layer? (new, and the gap Q1 was hiding)

Every implementation in the corpus hand-labels the events that drive the emotion engine: szabo-2011-emotional-engine through a MATLAB GUI, belle-2019-programming-with-affect through scripted dialogue choices. That is the appraisal step, and occ-model is the only model held that formalises it — desirability against goals, praiseworthiness against standards, appeal against tastes, with an explicit potential/threshold formalism (pp. 215–228).

Three candidate answers, none taken:

  1. Hand-authored rules — what the corpus did, what OCC recommends (p. 219), and what does not scale.
  2. Read off the planner — Gratch's Émile and EMA derive appraisal variables from planning representations. On robot-platform the analogue would be py_trees_ros blackboard state. See appraisal-theory.
  3. Learn it — consistent with rl-for-behaviour-learning, but nothing in the corpus does this and OCC argues against it.

Option 2 is the most interesting and the least evidenced. This is arguably a sharper open problem than Q1, because it is where every prior system stopped.

Q18 — Can a robot be said to have emotions, and does the thesis need it to?

ortony-2022-cognitive-structure p. 219 says no — robots "are simply not the kinds of things that can be conscious". machine-emotion-question argues the objection dissolves if the thesis uses functional rather than phenomenal language, which plutchik-model already supplies and function-orientedness already commits to.

Probably the cheapest open question in the wiki to close: a paragraph in the thesis introduction adopting functional language. Left open only because it is Csenge's call whether to state it explicitly or leave it implicit.

Q19 — Is body pose the wrong target for the perception gap?

multimodal-emotion-recognition has framed the gap as no source says how to read affect off a body pose. Gratch and Marsella state that "external expressions have only a loose connection to internal states" and that observers become accurate only when they also know the preceding events and the person's goals (ortony-2022-cognitive-structure p. 242, citing L. F. Barrett et al. and Ortony & Turner — both unheld).

If that is right, the 17-keypoint pipeline on mecanumbot-perception is necessary but not sufficient, and no amount of skeleton data closes the gap. It would make the robot's event and interaction history load-bearing rather than incidental — which is a larger architectural claim than the wiki has been making, and one that changes what the validation study has to measure. #needs-source
