It states outright that it is a summary and that "for references to other work cited in the paper the reader is referred to Plutchik (1980)". The reference list contains only Plutchik's own works. Every empirical result reported here — Block's semantic-differential study, Conte's paired comparisons, the chimpanzee EPI work — is second-hand and untraceable from this document. Takeaways

  • Emotion is a hypothetical construct, not a feeling report. Five arguments against defining emotion by verbal report: reports may deceive; repression creates false negatives; reports depend on conditioning history and verbal facility; emotions are attributed to infants, animals and the mentally ill who cannot report; and "emotions are rarely if ever experienced in a pure state".
  • Three languages for describing the same emotional state — subjective, behavioural, and functional. This is the most useful single contribution to this thesis; see the table below.
  • Eight basic prototype adaptive patterns, derived from ethological lists (Scott's ingestive, agonistic, sexual, shelter-seeking, care-giving, care-soliciting, eliminative, allelomimetic, investigative behaviours), modified for generality across phylogenetic levels: Incorporation, Rejection, Protection, Destruction, Reproduction, Reintegration, Orientation, Exploration.
  • Bipolar pairs: incorporation/rejection, protection/destruction, reproduction/reintegration, orientation/exploration.
  • Three structural dimensions — intensity (vertical), similarity (circular order), polarity (opposites) — giving the "emotion solid" cone (Fig. 1) and the wheel (Fig. 2) with primary dyads formed from adjacent pairs: love, submission, awe, disappointment, remorse, contempt, aggressiveness, optimism.
  • The causal chain (Tables 2 and 3): stimulus event → inferred cognition → feeling → behaviour → function. Threat → "danger" → fear → running → Protection. Loss of valued individual → "abandonment" → sadness → crying → Reintegration.
  • "Cognitions are in the service of emotions." Cognition evolved "in order to predict the future"; cognitive processes are instruments, means to an end.
  • Emotion as behavioural homeostasis — "emotions act to try to reestablish the conditions that existed before the situation arose".
  • Two functions of emotion: to communicate intentions or probable course of action to others (via displays, intention movements, gestures), and to increase survival chances in emergencies (via overt action).

Why this matters more than the wheel

The wheel and the dyads are what Plutchik is famous for, and they are the least useful part here. The functional column is the find. It is an emotion model whose primitives are behaviours defined by what they accomplish — which is function-orientedness arriving from emotion theory rather than from robotics, forty years early.

For a robot with no face that must express through motion, a model whose primitives are already behaviours-with-functions is a far better fit than one whose primitives are subcortical circuits. The behavioural column also maps directly onto the re-expression table in dog-human-interaction-model — withdrawing, attacking, stopping, examining are all available on robot-platform.

Emotion versus motivation — and Q2

Plutchik distinguishes them on five points. Emotions are aroused by external stimuli, by the presence of a survival-related event, have few natural objects, are induced after an object is seen or evaluated, and depend on environmental events that may occur randomly. Motivations are aroused by changing internal states, by the absence of homeostatically significant stimuli, have specific natural objects (food, water), are induced before search begins, and "tend to have a rhythmic character".

He allows that "internal stimuli may, under certain conditions, arouse emotional reactions" but treats the distinction as real.

This cuts against feeding battery state into the emotion model directly — on Plutchik's account that is motivation, not emotion. It converges with Panksepp's separation of homeostatic from emotional affects (panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness). Two independently derived models agreeing that internal state is a different category is the strongest signal Q2 has received.

Emotion and learning

Three influences (§Emotion and learning): an individual may learn to modify or inhibit the outward signs of emotion; experience affects which basic emotions are evident and which interact; and "the reinforcement history of an individual may affect the strength and stability of the emotion mixtures that develop".

But: "The basic emotions are not learned, but are a direct expression of the genetic potentials of an individual." That is a constraint on rl-for-behaviour-learning — on this model the state set is given, and only expression, mixture and strength are learnable. Consistent with keeping the emotional engine hand-authored and learning only the behaviour policy.

Method and limitations

A theoretical synthesis, not an empirical report. Reported evidence:

  • Block (1957), semantic differential on 15 emotions × 20 scales → circular configuration in two factors; Plutchik reports replicating with a larger sample.
  • Conte & Plutchik, modified paired comparisons over 140+ emotion words against three reference terms → angular locations; terms "distributed around the entire circle with no appreciable gaps".
  • The Emotions Profile Index (EPI), adapted for animals by Kellerman & Buirski and applied to 23 free-living chimpanzees (13 male, 10 female) at the Gombe Stream Research Centre, Tanzania — seven graduate-student raters, interjudge reliabilities mostly > +.70. Dominance rank correlated with emotion dimensions: dominant animals rated more distrustful and aggressive, nondominant more timid, impulsive and trustful.

The chimpanzee study is worth noting for validation-study: it is an instrument for rating emotional disposition in a non-verbal subject from behaviour alone, with reported inter-rater reliability — structurally the same measurement problem the human study faces from the other direction.

Limitations: 1982, pre-dating the affective-neuroscience literature entirely; no neural grounding; the empirical support is untraceable from this document (see warning above); and the eight primaries are justified by parsimony and utility rather than by decisive evidence — Plutchik lists his criteria openly as summarising power, ability to support a structural model, ability to guide research, and ability to relate emotion, personality and diagnosis.
