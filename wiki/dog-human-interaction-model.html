Was a stub with an unargued transfer claim. The corpus now supplies both the argument and, more usefully, concrete behaviour tables.

The argument, now sourced

miklosi-2017-ethorobotics makes the case properly: domestication (~18,800– 32,100 years) produced an agent morphologically and behaviourally very different from humans that nonetheless acquired enough social-competence to integrate into human society. Dogs and humans each retained independent evolutionary capacity, and competition between them is limited. Breeds occupy specific behavioural niches defined by function — the biological precedent for function-orientedness.

Dogs are also pragmatically suited as a model: their behaviour is unusually well-documented and catalogued; humans read dog emotional signals without specific prior experience; dog signals are partly redundant across modalities (posture, movement, ear set, tail carriage, vocalisation), which makes them unambiguous and allows a subset to be selected for a given robot (gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution).

The transfer claim is explicitly functional analogy, not imitation

Every source states this. lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution: "we do not want to copy dog behaviour in any direct sense. Our goal is to observe the behavioural organisation of various behaviour systems in dogs and build a functional analogue into our robots using multimodal channels." szabo-2011-emotional-engine: "we do not want to provide an emotional model of the dog. We consider the analogy as important when modelling the emotional space of an artificial agent." gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution: "we do not advocate to build robots that mimic a dog, as such attempts would constrain the behavioural repertoire and may lead to discomfort in some users."

This resolves step 4 of thesis: the claim is not that dog behaviour transfers literally, but that dog behaviour is a source of legible functional primitives that can be re-expressed in whatever embodiment is available.

Anger approaches crawling; wags tail; head up and down dynamically (barking), shows teeth approaches; moves arm high; swings arm several times low-pitched, noisy, loud, staccato

Source contexts: joy = greeting the owner / going for a walk; anger = facing a threatening stranger / watching a stranger through the fence; fear = facing a threatening stranger; sadness = separation from owner / being scolded; neutral = 'mobile owner' task.

Vocalisations follow Morton's rules, as dog barks do — the acoustic parameters map to emotional content independently of the sound's source.

Behavioural states (Kovács et al.)

MissTheOwner, AnxietyLevel, ExplorationLevel, driven by owner presence, owner-to-dog distance, stranger-to-dog distance, place familiarity — from the attachment-sst. See fuzzy-automaton-fri.

Leading interaction (Lakatos et al. demo)

Hearing-dog scenario, four components: interaction initiation, leading behaviour, feedback, goal-oriented attention-directing behaviour (lakatos-2014-embodiments-demo).

Re-reading the tables as an RL specification

Following rl-for-behaviour-learning, these tables stop being a lookup and become two different objects:

  • The expressive-behaviour table is the action set A(s). Its robot re-expression column is a list of named, ethologically-derived primitives — precisely what sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3 calls an animal's "instinctual impulses" and identifies with the set of admissible actions.
  • The event → impact table is a reward specification. It is already an expert-elicited mapping from context to signed, scaled change in affective state, which is structurally what a reward function is.

Two cautions. First, the impact table is valenced and five-dimensional, and a reward signal is scalar — Q14 in open-questions. Second, the transcription caveat below still applies; do not build a reward function on an unchecked table.

What remains open

  • Which characteristics survive on robot-platform? No tail, no ears, no arms, no vertical DOF. Of the re-expressions above, only approach, back, turn away, orient and motionless are directly available; arm and antenna signals are not. Q4 in open-questions.
  • Attachment is listed first among dog-derived skills by miklosi-2017-ethorobotics but no source here implements it beyond the MissTheOwner state.
  • The primary dog-ethology sources (Topál, Gácsi 2001, Pongrácz, Faragó) are all cited and none is in the corpus. See citation-backlog.
