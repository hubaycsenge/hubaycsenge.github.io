The framing discipline of this thesis, named and defined in miklosi-2017-ethorobotics: an interdisciplinary science relying on evolutionary, ecological and ethological concepts for developing social robots.

The core proposal: social robots should be regarded as separate species highly adapted to their niche, and their similarity to humans — in appearance or behaviour — is irrelevant in itself, without a function that requires it. Human-like features are permitted where they are optimal for the function (the paper gives simple verbal feedback and a human hand as examples), and only then.

The two commitments

  • interspecific-hri — the human–robot relationship is inter-specific, not intra-specific. Most likely a form of symbiosis in which humans experience positive fitness consequences.
  • function-orientedness — behaviour, embodiment and problem-solving ability should fit the robot's specific environment and function. Robot construction should start from function and environment.

Together these license dogs as the behavioural model species, and are motivated by the reinterpretation of the uncanny-valley.

The claimed advantages

From the robots' side: more efficient in their niche because unconstrained by expected human similarity; more acceptable social partners than imperfect androids given the state of the art; and no gender problem, since they fall in the category of animals where gender is secondary.

From the humans' side: no competition with robots (robots compete with each other); humans keep control by controlling the nature of interaction and whether the niche stays open; and humans already have the mental skills to adjust their social behaviour to differently-embodied agents that show basic social-competence.

The paper also frames robot evolution ecologically: robots "survive only if their niche exists and die out if they have not performed well to the expectation of humans".

Why it constrains the affective work

Emotion recognition and expression research overwhelmingly assumes human-like agents; importing it uncritically would violate function-orientedness (Q5 in open-questions). The corpus shows the alternative works — emotion-recognition-benchmarks records dog-derived body movement matching a human-like face without needing one.

Status

Well sourced now. The remaining weakness is not in the wiki but in the field: miklosi-2017-ethorobotics closes by listing its own open questions ("What is the minimally functioning social competence in ethorobots? Does it depend on embodiment and/or function?... there is a long way to go"), and the empirical literature answering them is small and produced almost entirely by one network (hungarian-ethorobotics-network).
