Two problems in the supplied list

1. The number [9] is used twice — once for Plutchik (1982) and once for Belle et al. (2019). The wiki has been treating the behaviour-tree citation as [10]. The thesis document needs renumbering; flagging it here so it is not discovered at submission.

2. [7] is IECON 2012, not 2011. The supplied entry gives IECON 2012 — 38th Annual Conference of the IEEE Industrial Electronics Society, pp. 5516–5522, doi:10.1109/iecon.2012.6389513. The wiki page is named szabo-2011-emotional-engine and dated 2011 because the raw filename says "Szabó 2011 IECON Canada.pdf". IECON 2012 was held in Montréal, Canada, which fits; IECON 2011 was in Melbourne. The PDF's own title page matches the 2012 entry exactly, author list included.

The filename is almost certainly wrong and the wiki inherited the error. The page's frontmatter has been corrected to 2012; the filename has been left alone because 22 pages link to it and log.md is append-only. Renaming is a mechanical follow-up if wanted — say the word.

Priority order — what is still missing

  1. [5] Wani et al. (2021) and [6] Li & Deng (2022). These are the sensor-based emotion analysis references, and they are surveys of speech emotion recognition and facial expression recognition respectively. Note what that means: the proposal's perception plan was face + voice, and robot-platform delivers body pose (mecanumbot-perception) with an unconfirmed audio path. Getting these two will document a route the platform does not currently support. The literature the thesis actually needs — skeleton-based / bodily affect recognition — is not in the reference list at all. See multimodal-emotion-recognition.
  2. The affect models behind belle-2019-programming-with-affect, all but one still unheld and all load-bearing for the comparison with prior art: ALMA (Gebhard) — the layered model of affect EMoBeT reimplements; ~~the OCC model~~ — ✅ held 2026-07-28 as ortony-2022-cognitive-structure, and in its 2nd edition (2022), not the 1988 one Belle et al. cite. See occ-model; GAMYGDALA (Popescu et al.) — an OCC-based black-box appraisal engine; Johansson & Dell'Acqua's emotional selector BT node; the Five Factor Model of personality; and the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve. See emotion-model-comparison and behaviour-trees.
  3. Plutchik (1980), Emotion: A psychoevolutionary synthesis — required to trace any empirical claim in plutchik-1982-psychoevolutionary, which cites only itself.

Highest value — RL on real robots

  • Modayil, J. & Sutton, R. S. (2014), Prediction driven behavior: Learning predictions that drive fixed responses, AAAI-14 Workshop on Artificial Intelligence and Robotics, Quebec City. Cited in §14.1 as "Pavlovian control": a mobile robot combining a fixed response with online prediction learning, explicitly not based on reward maximisation. Structurally the closest published thing to "ethologically-authored primitives plus a learned predictor". Get this one.

Opened by ortony-2022-cognitive-structure — 2026-07-28

A new frontier, and the first one in the wiki that is about computational appraisal rather than emotion theory. Priority order:

  1. L. F. Barrett et al. on the weak link between facial/bodily expression and internal state — cited at p. 242 for the claim that surface cues are insufficient. The highest-value acquisition in the wiki right now: if it holds, it reframes multimodal-emotion-recognition and Q19, and it bears on the thesis' central novelty claim.
  2. Sloman & Croucher (1981), Why robots will have emotions — the counter-position to OCC's denial that machines can have emotions, from within AI. Needed for machine-emotion-question / Q18. Also Frijda & Swagerman (1987) and Adolphs & Anderson (2018), cited in the same breath at p. 219.
  3. Gratch & Marsella on EMA — the domain-independent appraisal-and-coping model, in SOAR. The nearest thing in any literature to an implementable appraisal front end. Several papers; Marsella & Gratch's synthesis is the one to find. Bears on Q17 and appraisal-theory.
  4. Gratch's Émile and Elliott's Affective Reasoner — the two systems that bracket the hand-authored/derived distinction. Historical but load-bearing for the argument that appraisal need not be hand-coded.
  5. Ortony & Turner — cited at p. 242 alongside Barrett. Likely What's basic about basic emotions?, which would also bear on emotion-model-comparison. Unverified; do not cite until the reference list is checked. #needs-source
  6. Lazarus, Arnold, Scherer et al. — the appraisal tradition OCC sits in. Background rather than urgent; appraisal-theory currently rests on OCC's own characterisation of them, which is a single-source account of a whole field.

The reference list of ortony-2022-cognitive-structure (pp. 259–283) has not been mined systematically — only the works cited in the passages read closely. A pass over Ch. 10's references would likely yield more of the affective-computing frontier.

Opened by the ostension ingest — 2026-07-30

From topal-2008-perseverative and okumura-2020-ostension-attention. This frontier is about natural pedagogy and it feeds pedagogical-stance, Q20–Q22.

Blocking a question, not just enriching a page

  1. Topál, Gergely, Erdőhegyi, Csibra & Miklósi (2009), Differential sensitivity to human communication in dogs, wolves, and human infants, Science 325 — cited as "Topál et al. 2009a" in topal-2014-ostensive §11.3.1.3 for dogs selecting an empty container after ostensive cueing. Almost certainly the dog A-not-B study, i.e. the direct counterpart of topal-2008-perseverative with overlapping authors. This is the highest-value acquisition in the wiki right now: it is the only thing that can answer Q20, which currently blocks the pipeline design. Volume, page numbers and exact title unverified — do not cite until the record is checked. #needs-source
  2. Okumura, Kanakogi, Kanda, Ishiguro & Itakura (2013) — the robot-gaze studies, cited in okumura-2020-ostension-attention as 2013a and 2013b. Infants followed a robot's gaze but did not learn from it. This is the only evidence in the wiki on robot-produced ostension and it is negative, so Q22 cannot be argued until it is held and read properly. Probable venues: Developmental Science / PLoS ONE / RO-MAN — unverified. Also Boyer, Pan & Bertenthal (2011), cited alongside it.

The theory, currently held only second-hand

  1. Csibra & Gergely (2006), in Processes of Change in Brain and Cognitive Development, OUP, pp. 249–274 — the primary statement of the pedagogical stance and the interpretive bias of generalizability. Everything on pedagogical-stance rests on this and the wiki has it only through two papers that cite it. Also Gergely & Csibra (2006) in Roots of Human Sociality, Berg, pp. 229–255, and Csibra & Gergely (2009, 2011) on natural pedagogy.
  2. Yoon, Johnson & Csibra (2008), PNAS 105:13690 — ostensive context shifts infants to encoding object identity over location. Cited by both new sources, and it is the cleanest existing manipulation of what gets encoded rather than whether attention moved. Directly models the generic/episodic split.

The opposing camp — get it, per the network problem

  1. Szufnarowska, Rohlfing, Fawcett & Gredebäck (2014) and Gredebäck, Astor & Fawcett (2018) — attention modulation theory, the position that ostensive cues are merely attention-grabbing. okumura-2020-ostension-attention rebuts them and the wiki currently knows them only through that rebuttal. Given how much of the corpus is hungarian-ethorobotics-network, holding the opposing position in the original matters more here than usual. Also De Bordes, Cox, Hasselman & Cillessen (2013).
  2. Senju & Csibra (2008) — 6-month-olds follow gaze more after ostensive cues; the study the debate above is about.

Lower priority

  1. Gergely, Egyed & Király (2007), Dev. Sci. 10:139; Egyed, Király & Gergely (2013); Okumura, Kobayashi & Itakura (2016, 2017) — the encoding-strategy line.
  2. Ruffman & Langman (2002) and Longo & Berthental (2006) — the attentional-bias and mirror-neuron accounts topal-2008-perseverative argues against. Needed only to report that argument fairly.
  3. Fukuyama et al. (2015) — caregivers exaggerate demonstrations dynamically. Relevant to whether the video-based results of okumura-2020-ostension-attention transfer to live interaction, which is the condition a robot actually operates in.
