Two problems in the supplied list

1. The number [9] is used twice — once for Plutchik (1982) and once for Belle et al. (2019). The wiki has been treating the behaviour-tree citation as [10]. The thesis document needs renumbering; flagging it here so it is not discovered at submission.

2. [7] is IECON 2012, not 2011. The supplied entry gives IECON 2012 — 38th Annual Conference of the IEEE Industrial Electronics Society, pp. 5516–5522, doi:10.1109/iecon.2012.6389513. The wiki page is named szabo-2011-emotional-engine and dated 2011 because the raw filename says "Szabó 2011 IECON Canada.pdf". IECON 2012 was held in Montréal, Canada, which fits; IECON 2011 was in Melbourne. The PDF's own title page matches the 2012 entry exactly, author list included.

The filename is almost certainly wrong and the wiki inherited the error. The page's frontmatter has been corrected to 2012; the filename has been left alone because 22 pages link to it and log.md is append-only. Renaming is a mechanical follow-up if wanted — say the word.

Priority order — what is still missing

  1. [5] Wani et al. (2021) and [6] Li & Deng (2022). These are the sensor-based emotion analysis references, and they are surveys of speech emotion recognition and facial expression recognition respectively. Note what that means: the proposal's perception plan was face + voice, and robot-platform delivers body pose (mecanumbot-perception) with an unconfirmed audio path. Getting these two will document a route the platform does not currently support. The literature the thesis actually needs — skeleton-based / bodily affect recognition — is not in the reference list at all. See multimodal-emotion-recognition.
  2. The affect models behind belle-2019-programming-with-affect, none held and all now load-bearing for the comparison with prior art: ALMA (Gebhard) — the layered model of affect EMoBeT reimplements; the OCC model (Ortony, Clore & Collins) — a third emotion model in the corpus alongside Panksepp and Plutchik, and the one game AI actually uses; GAMYGDALA (Popescu et al.) — an OCC-based black-box appraisal engine; Johansson & Dell'Acqua's emotional selector BT node; the Five Factor Model of personality; and the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve. See emotion-model-comparison and behaviour-trees.
  3. Plutchik (1980), Emotion: A psychoevolutionary synthesis — required to trace any empirical claim in plutchik-1982-psychoevolutionary, which cites only itself.

Counter-evidence — highest value

  • Petisca, Dias & Paiva (2014) — robot emotion expression sometimes worsens human perception. Directly qualifies step 1 of thesis. Q8.
  • The paper that "argue[s] with its effectiveness" — ref [35] of lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution, apparently a critique of the dog model for HRI. Given that the corpus is one research network (hungarian-ethorobotics-network), independent criticism is the most valuable thing missing.
  • Mori (1970) and the uncanny-valley empirical literature — Mathur & Reichling (2016), Ferrey et al. (2015), MacDorman et al. See uncanny-valley.

Reinforcement learning — from sutton-barto-2018-rl

References cited in Ch. 14–15 and §17.4–17.5, none held. Priority reflects the decision recorded in rl-for-behaviour-learning. Full details are in the book's reference list, so these citekeys are safe to chase but should be verified against the originals.

Highest value — RL on real robots

  • Modayil, J. & Sutton, R. S. (2014), Prediction driven behavior: Learning predictions that drive fixed responses, AAAI-14 Workshop on Artificial Intelligence and Robotics, Quebec City. Cited in §14.1 as "Pavlovian control": a mobile robot combining a fixed response with online prediction learning, explicitly not based on reward maximisation. Structurally the closest published thing to "ethologically-authored primitives plus a learned predictor". Get this one.
  • Modayil, J., White, A. & Sutton, R. S. (2014), Multi-timescale nexting in a reinforcement learning robot, Adaptive Behavior 22(2):146–160.

Coverage gaps by area

  • Sensor-based affect recognition — nothing. The central chapter has no literature. Critical.
  • Datasets and benchmarks — wiki/datasets/ is empty; no ingested source uses a public corpus.
  • Behaviour trees in HRI — still essentially nothing. colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees covers autonomous driving, industrial robotics and the Amazon Picking Challenge; its only social-robot entry is a single paragraph on the JIBO SDK. No interaction design, no human-subject results, no affect.
  • Robot internal state / homeostatic drives in affect models — nothing. Kismet's "drives" are the nearest concept and only reach the wiki second-hand. The intrinsic-motivation references above are the nearest formal literature.
  • Learning in ethologically-grounded robots — nothing. sutton-barto-2018-rl supplies the algorithms and no source in the corpus applies them to an ethological behaviour model, which is either an opportunity or a sign the combination has been tried elsewhere and should be searched for.
  • Independent replication of the dog model — nothing.
  • Long-term / longitudinal HRI — nothing; every study is single-session, though social-competence is defined as emerging over time.
