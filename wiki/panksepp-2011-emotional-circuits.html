Jaak Panksepp, The basic emotional circuits of mammalian brains: Do animals have affective lives?, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews 35(9):1791–1804, 2011. doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2011.08.003

A Festschrift essay for a special issue celebrating Panksepp's cross-species affective neuroscience at Bowling Green State University. Shorter and later than panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness; read both, cite the 2005 paper for theory and this one for the per-circuit descriptions.

Not on the research-proposal reference list — Csenge supplied it alongside the 2005 paper.

Takeaways

  • Thumbnail descriptions of all seven circuits, the most usable per-system summary in the corpus. See panksepp-affective-systems for the table.
  • A three-level hierarchy: primary-process (evolutionary "tools for living"), secondary-process (unconscious learning and memory), tertiary-process (higher cognition). Mixing levels is his central methodological complaint.
  • Seven defining attributes of an emotional system (Fig. 4), including that "emotions outlast the stimuli that activated the systems" — which is exactly the temporal-dynamics layer szabo-2011-emotional-engine built by hand with peak widths and decline rates, and it now has a source.
  • The "Law of Affect": "'rewards' and 'punishments' would not work unless they changed the way animals feel affectively." Affect is proposed as the mechanism of reinforcement, not a by-product of it. Bears directly on Q15 in open-questions — it is an argument for the emotional engine being the reward function rather than a state feature.
  • PLAY builds social competence: "A key function of the social PLAY system is to help young animals to acquire the social knowledge and refinement of subtle social interactions that they will need to thrive... may be one of the major emotional forces that promotes the epigenetic construction of higher social brain functions." See social-competence.
  • SEEKING wants to learn but wants nothing in particular: "this system does not 'want' anything specific before learning; it just wants opportunities to explore the world... It should be emphasized that this system 'wants' to learn, is designed to learn automatically."
  • Decortication leaves emotional-instinctual urges intact while severely impairing instrumental learning; "many forms of classical conditioning survive". That maps onto the prediction/control split in conditioning.
  • Repeats the RPE objection recorded on panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness.

Two corrections this forces on the wiki

Nobody has mapped the inter-circuit interactions

"All primal emotional systems interact with each other in primal inhibitory and excitatory relationships that no one has yet mapped empirically."

panksepp-affective-systems previously claimed that "inter-circuit inhibition gives a principled basis for arbitration rules". It does not — there is no map to base rules on. Corrected on that page; bears on emotion-arbitration.

The affective-neuroscience literature on dogs is essentially empty. Panksepp reports his own Pubmed scan: 142,392 hits for "emotions", dropping to 4,293 with "brain" added, 616 for rats, 205 for mice — and 5 for dogs. For a thesis grounding a robot's affect model in dog ethology, that is a structural gap: the neuroscience is rat-based, the ethology is dog-based, and nothing bridges them.

Method and limitations

Same as panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness — a single-author review of ESB/CSB, self-stimulation and place-preference work, mostly in rats, heavily self-cited. Additionally:

  • Festschrift genre. Many references are "in this issue" or "in press", i.e. not independently established at time of writing.
  • Openly polemical — "ruthless reductionism", "neuroscientific hubris".
  • Peer-reviewed journal, but a review essay rather than a report of new data.
