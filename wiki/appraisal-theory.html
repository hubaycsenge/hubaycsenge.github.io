The family of emotion theories holding that emotions arise from an individual's cognitive evaluation of how their goals and preferences are impacted by external circumstances — not from the stimulus itself, but from its construal. occ-model is the member of this family that affective computing adopted; ortony-2022-cognitive-structure Ch. 10 names Arnold and Scherer et al. as others in the tradition, and Lazarus as the psychologist whose work made appraisal mainstream. (Those three are unheld — see citation-backlog.)

The core commitment: the same event produces different emotions in different agents, and the difference is explained by the agent's value system, not by the event. An appraisal theory therefore has two halves — a set of appraisal variables computed from the situation, and a mapping from variable patterns to affective states.

Why this matters to a robot architecture

Appraisal theory is the only tradition in the corpus that takes the path from situation to emotion as its subject. Both other models in the wiki are theories of what the states are:

  • panksepp-affective-systems grounds states in neural circuits, and says what activates each circuit only in broad terms.
  • plutchik-model grounds states in adaptive function, and maps each to a behaviour, but the trigger side is thin.
  • Appraisal theory is almost entirely about the trigger side, and almost silent on what the emotion then does.

The wiki has been treating Q1 as a choice of state set. Appraisal theory shows that is only half the engine, and the half the corpus has said least about. See emotion-model-comparison.

Appraisal variables as by-products of competent agency

The most useful idea for this thesis comes from Gratch and Marsella's chapter in ortony-2022-cognitive-structure (Ch. 10, pp. 235–237). Early appraisal systems hand-authored rules mapping situations to appraisals — Elliott's Affective Reasoner would encode "if a team scores and the person is a fan, the event is desirable". This obviously does not scale.

Their argument for what replaced it: appraisals "summarize key judgments that any intelligent system would need to make to act adaptively in the world". A system with goals must already assess goal congruence; a system acting under uncertainty must already assess likelihood; a system in a social setting must already reason about norms. So appraisal variables can be read off an agent's existing planning machinery rather than authored separately. Gratch's Émile derives them from classical AI planning representations; EMA generalises this into a domain-independent model of appraisal and coping, implemented in SOAR.

This is the route past the hand-authoring objection

ortony-2022-cognitive-structure states a preference for hand-coding cognition–emotion relationships over learning them (p. 219), and szabo-2011-emotional-engine and belle-2019-programming-with-affect both did exactly that. But Gratch and Marsella's move is a third option, and neither hand-authoring nor learning: derive the appraisal variables from representations the agent needs anyway.

On robot-platform the analogues exist. A py_trees behaviour tree already encodes goals and their status; navigation already computes reachability, which is a control estimate; the perception stack already produces detections with confidences, which is a likelihood estimate. Whether desirability, praiseworthiness and appeal can be read off py_trees_ros blackboard state is an open engineering question and a good candidate for an early experiment. #needs-source

It also bears directly on Q2: if appraisal variables are read off internal planning state, then battery and motor health are already in the same representation as the goal statuses that produce event-based appraisals — which would make them commensurable by construction rather than by a conversion the wiki has not been able to specify. Speculative; recorded as a direction. See open-questions.

Coping, and appraisal as a loop

Appraisal theories in the Lazarus line do not stop at generating an emotion. The cycle is appraisal → coping → reappraisal: the emotion prompts a response, the response changes the situation or the agent's construal of it, and the situation is appraised again.

EMA makes coping selection depend on the control appraisal: high perceived control selects problem-focused coping (act on the situation), low control selects emotion-focused coping (act on one's own beliefs, attention or goals) (ortony-2022-cognitive-structure p. 239). Gratch and Marsella describe their own theoretical movement over time as going from coping-as-a-side-effect to coping as the central control mechanism for a social agent — emotion and decision-making as "different views on the same underlying mechanism".

That is a strong claim and it lands close to this thesis: it says the emotional engine is not a module hanging off the behaviour layer, it is the behaviour layer's control policy. Compare rl-for-behaviour-learning, which reaches a structurally similar conclusion from a completely different direction, and emotion-arbitration.

Reverse appraisal

If observers know the mapping from situations and values to emotions, they can run it backwards: infer an agent's goals and standards from its emotional displays (ortony-2022-cognitive-structure p. 243). Demonstrated to change human decisions in social tasks, including with card-playing robots whose displays let players infer their dispositions.

This gives emotion-attribution a mechanism rather than just an observed effect — people are not merely projecting, they are solving for the agent's value system. It also implies expressive behaviour is informative, not decorative, and that a robot expressing an emotion it does not act consistently with will teach observers a value system it does not have.

Limits and objections

  • Cognitivist by construction. Appraisal presumes evaluation. Panksepp's position — that affect is generated subcortically and does not require cognitive appraisal — is a direct challenge, and ortony-2022-cognitive-structure does not engage with it.
  • Object-based emotions are the authors' own exception. OCC states Attraction emotions are experienced "more or less directly" and "tend to have little or no interesting cognitive basis", because tastes are "cognitively unanalyzable and irreducible" (Ch. 8). So even within OCC, one of the three branches is not really appraisal-driven — and it is the branch that covers how people feel about robots.
  • Anthropocentric. Goals, standards, tastes and responsibility attribution are human value structures. Whether they transfer to interspecific-hri is untested.
