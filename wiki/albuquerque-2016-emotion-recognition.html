Natalia Albuquerque, Kun Guo, Anna Wilkinson, Carine Savalli, Emma Otta & Daniel Mills, Dogs recognize dog and human emotions, Biology Letters 12(1):20150883, January 2016. doi:10.1098/rsbl.2015.0883

Reference [4] of research-proposal, now held. A short peer-reviewed empirical report — and, unusually for this corpus, from outside the Hungarian network (Lincoln, São Paulo, Federal University of São Paulo). See hungarian-ethorobotics-network; independent evidence has been the single most valuable missing thing in the wiki.

The claim

These results demonstrate that dogs can extract and integrate bimodal sensory emotional information, and discriminate between positive and negative emotions from both humans and dogs.

The authors describe this as "the first evidence of the integration of heterospecific emotional expressions in a species other than humans".

Method

Cross-modal preferential looking, no familiarisation phase. N = 17 healthy socialised adult family dogs, various breeds.

Two screens showed two grey-scale images of the same individual — one happy/playful, one angry/aggressive — while a single vocalisation played: a dog bark or a human voice, of positive or negative valence, from that same individual; or Brownian noise as a neutral control. Stimuli featured one female and one male of each species. Models were unfamiliar, and the human speech was in an unfamiliar language (Brazilian Portuguese) to rule out prior experience with model identity or lexical content.

Two 10-trial sessions two weeks apart; each trial 5 s, valid only if the dog looked at the images for ≥ 2.5 s. 20 trials = 4 face-pairs × 2 vocalisations × 2 positions, plus 4 neutral-sound controls, so each dog saw each combination once.

Measure: congruence index = (C − I) / T, where C and I are time looking at the congruent and incongruent face and T is total looking time. Analysed with a GLMM, individual dog as random effect; valence, stimulus sex, stimulus species and presentation side as fixed effects; backward selection.

Results

Finding Value
Preference for congruent face 67% of trials (n = 188)
Mean congruence index 0.19 ± 0.03, t₁₆ = 5.53, p < 0.0001
Dog faces 0.26 ± 0.05
Human faces 0.12 ± 0.05
Species difference F₁,₁₇₀ = 4.42, p = 0.04
Neutral-sound control 0.04 ± 0.07, t₁₆ = 0.56, p = 0.58 — not significant

The effect held across valence (negative p < 0.0001, positive p = 0.005), stimulus sex and presentation side. After backward selection only stimulus species survived in the final model.

Why this matters here

1. It is the evidential base for step 1 of thesis, from the dog side. The wiki has strong evidence that humans attribute emotion to robots (emotion-attribution). This is evidence that dogs genuinely recognise emotion — categorically, not just discriminatively. The authors are careful about that distinction: prior work showing dogs respond differently to happy and angry faces "could be attributed solely to learning of contiguous visual features", whereas cross-modal integration "requires internal categorical representation" and so demonstrates recognition rather than differentiation.

2. It licenses the multimodal assumption. The perception of emotion "through cross-modal sensory integration enables faster, more accurate and more reliable recognition". That is a sourced argument for multimodal-emotion-recognition being multimodal, which the wiki previously took on faith from research-proposal.

The conspecific advantage is a problem for the design

Dogs were more than twice as sensitive to conspecific emotional cues as to human ones (0.26 vs 0.12). The authors attribute this to "a more refined mechanism for the categorization of emotional information from conspecifics".

The thesis proposes a robot that reads human affect using a dog-derived model. This result says that the dog's own competence is weakest exactly where the robot's task lies. It does not defeat the design — cross-species recognition is real and significant — but it caps expectations, and it predicts that a faithfully dog-modelled recogniser should be worse at humans than at its own kind. Worth stating in the thesis before a reviewer does. (Reading of the result; the authors draw no robotics conclusion.)

3. Faces and voices, on a robot with neither the sensor nor the output for faces. The evidenced dog capacity is face + vocalisation. robot-platform sees body pose, not faces (mecanumbot-perception), and its audio path is unconfirmed. So this paper establishes that the capacity being modelled is bimodal facial-vocal, while the platform implements neither modality. That mismatch should be named rather than glossed.

Limitations

  • N = 17, single laboratory, one session pair.
  • Looking time is an indirect measure. It indexes attention to congruence, not the dog's own emotional state or any behavioural response.
  • Static grey-scale images and single vocalisations — not natural interaction. The authors note this as a strength for their argument (no temporal contiguity, so learned association is unlikely) but it limits ecological validity.
  • Two human models only, one female one male.
  • Emotions tested are a single valence contrast (happy/playful versus angry/aggressive), not the five-state sets used elsewhere in the corpus (emotion-recognition-benchmarks). No fear, sadness or surprise.
  • Data are open on Dryad (doi:10.5061/dryad.tn888).
