The system this thesis builds: recognise events in context and simulate an emotional state for the robot which drives feedback research-proposal.

After ingesting the corpus, the picture has changed substantially: most of this architecture already exists, and the contribution has to be located precisely.

The canonical structure

Every emotion-handling robot in the corpus shares one structure (szabo-2010-emotional-engine):

input devices → event identification → EMOTIONAL ENGINE → output peripheries
 (visual, audio,   (events carrying      (interprets, computes    (expression)
  mechanical)       emotional info)       impact in the model)

Szabó et al. add input middleware and output middleware either side of the abstract model, so the same engine can drive different hardware. Worth adopting directly for robot-platform — the LED service and cmd_vel become output middleware; lidar and leg detection become input middleware.

An independent architecture that converges

belle-2019-programming-with-affect builds EMoBeT from OCC emotions, a PAD mood space and Five Factor personality — no contact with this lineage — and arrives at the same three-layer temporal structure: emotion (short) → mood (mid) → personality (long), against Szabó's short / medium / long-term layers. Two independent derivations of the same design is the strongest support the layered structure has.

Two things EMoBeT has that this lineage lacks:

  • Episodic memory of specific individuals, decaying on an Ebbinghaus curve. On re-meeting a person the agent recalls its strongest memory of them and re-triggers those emotions. Nothing in the Hungarian corpus does this, and it is what a longitudinal study of social-competence would need (Q16).
  • A retained long-term layer. Szabó's long-term layer appears in the 2010 paper and is dropped by 2012; EMoBeT keeps personality as a first-class subsystem.

What it does not have: any sensing, any learning, any internal state of the agent, and any evaluation.

Where the contribution actually is

Reframing after ingest

research-proposal claims novelty in "real-time event detection in real environments". The corpus supports something sharper and more defensible:

Every emotional engine in this lineage has been driven by hand-labelled events, and every human study of it has been wizard-of-oz. The models are mature; the perception-to-emotion link has never been closed. This thesis closes it.

Second, and possibly stronger: no prior model takes the robot's own state as input. Fusing proprioceptive-style signals with social perception is unattempted here, and it is what makes the robot's emotion its own rather than a mirror of the human's.

Inputs — two streams

1. Perceived state of surrounding people. See multimodal-emotion-recognition. The platform provides 17-keypoint body pose from a camera, fused with lidar detections into people in map space (mecanumbot-perception) — so the input is bodily: posture, orientation, gesture, gait, spatial arrangement. Richer than the attachment-sst-derived states the fuzzy automaton uses, and still not facial expression, which keeps Q5 answered. What is missing is any mapping from pose to affect — that gap is literature, not hardware.

2. The robot's own internal state — battery charge, sensor and motor states research-proposal. The telemetry exists (confirmed 2026-07-28, mecanumbot-repos): OpenCRState.msg publishes goal and measured wheel velocities, positions, currents and accelerations, per-wheel error states, DMS distance, battery voltage and 9-axis IMU; orin_battery_state adds INA219 power data. Per-wheel fault state is a real "how am I doing" signal rather than a bare charge level. Q2 is now a modelling question only.

Q2 now has a formalism, if not an answer

sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3 makes what is rewarding state-dependent, and the state explicitly includes "what is internal to this machine": "an animal will be more rewarded by eating when it is hungry than when it has just finished a satisfying meal". §17.4/§17.5 go further — reward may depend on motivational states, memories, or on the progress of learning itself (intrinsic motivation).

That gives a third option beside modulator, separate channel and mapped into the same space: internal state as a modulator of the reward function rather than of the affect space. It does not solve the commensurability problem, but it moves it somewhere with a literature. See reward-design.

Output

Emotional state → emotion-arbitration → expressive behaviour, executed via behaviour-trees (py_trees_ros), shaped by dog-human-interaction-model, constrained by function-orientedness, expressed through omnidirectional motion and four RGB LEDs.

Where the engine sits once behaviour is learnt

Following the decision recorded in rl-for-behaviour-learning, the engine is not the learner. It stays hand-authored and sits on the environment side of the RL agent boundary, computing the reward that trains the behaviour policy — which is where sutton-barto-2018-rl §3.1 says a reward computation belongs, "beyond [the agent's] ability to change arbitrarily". The boundary is drawn inside the robot, not at its shell: motors, sensors and the engine are all environment.

Whether the emotional state is the reward or a state feature is not settled — Q15 in open-questions.

Design questions still live

  • Does internal state modulate the affect space, occupy its own channel, or map into it? (Q2)
  • Should ostensive-signalling gate responses, so the robot reacts to events addressed to it rather than to all detected affect?
  • Fuzzy automaton or behaviour tree, or both? (Q9)
  • What latency budget makes "real-time" true? (Q3)
  • Does the medium-term layer feed arbitration, which Szabó left undone?
  • Recover the long-term layer from szabo-2010-emotional-engine as temperament — it converges with the "behavioural factors" of ichikawa-numakunai-2012-roman.
  • What collapses the five-dimensional state to a scalar reward? (Q14, see reward-design — the sharpest question this architecture creates.)

The missing front end — 2026-07-28

ortony-2022-cognitive-structure exposes a stage this page has not named. The canonical structure above runs events → emotional state → expression, but says nothing about how an event becomes an appraised event. Every implementation in the corpus filled that stage by hand: szabo-2011-emotional-engine with a MATLAB GUI, belle-2019-programming-with-affect with scripted dialogue choices.

occ-model formalises the stage — desirability against goals, praiseworthiness against standards, appeal against tastes — and gives a two-stage potential-then-threshold rule form (Ch. 9). appraisal-theory gives the route past hand-authoring: derive the appraisal variables from representations the agent needs anyway, as Émile and EMA do from planning state.

Recorded as Q17 in open-questions — arguably a sharper open problem than Q1, because it is where every prior system in the corpus stopped.

Contradiction — OCC denies that this engine can exist

"We do not consider it possible for computers to have any kind of experience, emotional or otherwise… computational artifacts, be they robots or virtual characters, are simply not the kinds of things that can be conscious" (ortony-2022-cognitive-structure p. 219). OCC is built to predict and explain human emotions, not to give a robot its own.

The engine on this page does the latter. The two positions are compatible only if the engine's states are claimed functionally rather than phenomenally — which costs nothing and is what function-orientedness already implies. Argued in full on machine-emotion-question; tracked as Q18.

The opposing camp — Sloman & Croucher (1981), Why robots will have emotions — is cited by OCC and unheld. See citation-backlog.
