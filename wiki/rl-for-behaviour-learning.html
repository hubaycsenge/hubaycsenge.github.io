The decision. Csenge, 2026-07-28: reinforcement learning algorithms are to be used to teach robot-platform behaviour patterns. This page is the design argument that follows, built on sutton-barto-2018-rl and on what the rest of the corpus already constrains. It answers Q11 in open-questions.

Everything here is either cited or marked as inference. Nothing in the corpus outside Sutton & Barto uses learning at all, so there is no prior art in this lineage to inherit from — the arguments below are constructed, not reported.

The objection that has to be answered first

Both existing architectures in the corpus treat hand-authored rules as a virtue, not a limitation:

Even after numerical optimization of the model, the rules are still human readable, and helps the formal validation of the model by the ethological experts. — kovacs-fri-automaton-series, via fuzzy-automaton-fri

szabo-2011-emotional-engine likewise elicits its event-impact table from experts. A learned policy is opaque; ethologists cannot validate it; and the whole methodological claim of ethorobotics is that behaviour should be grounded in observed animal behaviour rather than in whatever an optimiser finds.

This is a real objection and it is not answered by asserting that learning is modern.

The answer: learn over the ethological repertoire, not instead of it

The cats selected actions from those that they instinctively perform in their current situation, which Thorndike called their "instinctual impulses"... This is like the feature of our formalism where the action selected from a state s belongs to a set of admissible actions, A(s). Specifying these sets is an important aspect of reinforcement learning because it can radically simplify learning. They are like an animal's instinctual impulses. — sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3, see conditioning

The same book adds: "Nothing about reinforcement learning implies a tabula rasa view of learning and behavior. Indeed, experience with engineering applications has highlighted the importance of building into reinforcement learning systems knowledge that is analogous to what evolution provides to animals" (§14.7).

So the ethologically-derived behaviour repertoire becomes A(s). The re-expressions tabulated in dog-human-interaction-model — approach, back, turn away, orient, motionless, and their LED analogues — are the admissible actions. They stay hand-authored, ethologist-validatable, and legible. What is learnt is the selection and parameterisation over that set: which primitive, when, how strongly, in which context.

What this buys, and what it costs

Buys: every action the robot can take remains one an ethologist wrote down and can defend. The policy is a mapping into a small, named, meaningful action set, so it is inspectable in a way a policy over raw cmd_vel would not be. The objection above is substantially defused — legibility lives in A(s), not in the update rule.

Costs: the learned selection is still opaque, and the claim "this behaviour is ethologically grounded" now holds of the primitives but not of their sequencing. Sequencing is exactly what fuzzy-automaton-fri currently encodes from observed dog behaviour. That is a genuine loss and the thesis has to say so rather than paper over it.

(This framing is inference from §14.3 and §14.7, applied to this wiki. Sutton & Barto say nothing about robots, dogs or ethology.)

Proposed layering

Three layers, with the RL boundary drawn where §3.1 says to draw it — "the limit of the agent's absolute control, not of its knowledge":

  perception          →  EMOTIONAL ENGINE  →   BEHAVIOUR POLICY   →  actuation
  (lidar, leg det.,      (state model +        (learnt: actor over    (mecanum
   internal telemetry)    reward computation)   A(s), critic on V)     drive, LEDs)
  ─────── environment side ───────────────────┤├──── agent ────┤├─ environment ─
  • The emotional engine is not learnt. It stays as szabo-2011-emotional-engine built it, and it sits on the environment side of the agent boundary, where it computes the reward. §3.1 is explicit that the reward computation is external to the agent "because it defines the task facing the agent and thus must be beyond its ability to change arbitrarily" — which is also the engineering reason not to let one loop learn both.
  • The behaviour policy is learnt, as an actor over A(s) with a critic predicting return. See actor-critic for why this architecture and not another.
  • py_trees_ros becomes the executor of primitives, not the arbitrator — each leaf is one member of A(s). This partly dissolves Q9: fuzzy automaton versus behaviour tree stops being a contest if the tree executes and the policy selects. See behaviour-trees.

The BT layer is now sourced — and it does more than execute

colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees Ch. 8 describes RL-BT: a hand-designed BT in which selected nodes are replaced by Learning Nodes — either a Learning Action node wrapping Q-learning over a local state/action/reward, or a Learning Control Flow node, a Fallback that learns the order to try its own children.

§8.4 states the point:

"If RL-BT was started from scratch with a BT consisting of a single action, there would be no difference between RL-BT and standard RL. Instead, the point of combining BTs with RL is to address the curse of dimensionality, and do RL on smaller state spaces, while the BT connects these subproblems in a modular way."

Two independent arguments now compose. sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3 says the ethological repertoire should be the admissible action set A(s); Colledanchise & Ögren say the tree should carve the learning problem into local subproblems. Neither source makes the other's point. Together they give a layering in which the ethology supplies which primitives exist and the tree supplies how the learning is decomposed between them — which is a considerably better-founded architecture than the first draft of this page had.

The authors are explicit that RL-BT demands more designer input than plain RL — the tree structure plus states, actions and local rewards per learning node. For this thesis that is a feature: hand-authored structure is where the ethology lives.

Safety has a mechanism now — use it from the start

sutton-barto-2018-rl §17.5 names safe embedding of RL agents in physical environments as an open problem and offers nothing. colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees §8.2 offers Sequence(T_safe, T_learn) — a hand-authored safety subtree that overrides the learned tree, with a conservative margin so it can act in time.

For a robot learning around untrained humans in a real environment this belongs in the architecture from day one, not retrofitted before the human study. Note the chattering failure mode (§3.4).

17 keypoints per person is not a state representation

Raw pose is far too high-dimensional to learn over directly, and sutton-barto-2018-rl §1.4 explicitly declines the problem — "our concern in this book is not with designing the state signal". §14.2.4 is the relevant lesson instead: in the TD model, the stimulus representation determines the behaviour of the model more than the learning rule does (td-learning).

So the design effort goes into reducing keypoints to a small set of ethologically meaningful features — body orientation relative to the robot, postural openness, height/crouch, approach gait, arm extension — rather than into algorithm choice. That reduction is a thesis contribution in its own right and has no precedent in the corpus.

Two structural warnings from the book that this formulation must survive:

  • The Markov property is a restriction on the state, not on the process (§3.1). Proxemic features are a partial observation of an interaction whose relevant history includes what happened minutes ago. §17.3 treats state construction from non-Markov observations, and the book otherwise declines the problem: "our concern in this book is not with designing the state signal" (§1.4).
  • Online, incremental learning on physical systems is unsolved by the authors' own assessment (§17.5), and safe embedding of RL agents in physical environments is named "one of the most pressing areas for future research". Any claim that this is routine engineering is not supported by this source.

How learning happens: shaping, not deployment

Sparse reward is the expected failure mode — a single human encounter may yield one evaluative event (§17.4). The book's principal remedy is shaping: start from a reward signal that is dense given current behaviour and gradually modify it toward the target (reward-design).

This is also convenient in a way that should be stated openly rather than discovered later: shaping is training, and miklosi-2017-ethorobotics already lists "responsiveness to learning and training" among the dog-derived properties a socially competent robot must have (social-competence). A robot trained by shaping is not importing an alien machine-learning practice into an ethological framework — it is satisfying a requirement the framework already stated. This is the strongest in-corpus justification for the decision, and it was sitting unremarked on social-competence before this ingest.

The three questions this creates

Q14 — what collapses the emotional state to a scalar reward? The reward hypothesis needs one number (§3.2); the emotional state is a five-dimensional vector. The book acknowledges scalar reward is "at odds with" separate appetitive and aversive systems and passes over the problem (§14 terminology). This is the central unsolved design decision. See reward-design.

Q15 — is the emotional engine the reward function, or a state feature? Two readable architectures. Either the engine computes R and the policy never sees the emotion vector, or the emotion vector is part of S and reward comes from somewhere else (task success, human response). The first makes emotion motivationally efficacious; the second makes it merely informative. They predict different behaviour and the choice is not forced by anything in the corpus.

Q16 — should the shift from goal-directed to habitual control be a measured outcome? habitual-vs-goal-directed gives a documented mechanism and an instrument (outcome devaluation) for the one construct social-competence defines and no study in the corpus tests. Attractive, and it would require a longitudinal design with no precedent here.

A third vote against, from the appraisal tradition — 2026-07-28

Contradiction — OCC prefers hand-coding, explicitly

"While it is possible that machine learning techniques could lead to the development of these prerequisites, we believe that hand-coding the kinds of representations and cognition–emotion relationships that we have outlined is likely to be at least as effective" (ortony-2022-cognitive-structure p. 219).

How much weight it carries. Less than it looks. The claim is offered as a belief, with no evidence, no comparison and no benchmark, in a monograph whose empirical subject is human emotion rather than learning algorithms. It is also about the appraisal layer — how situations map to emotions — not about the behaviour policy this page is concerned with. On its own terms it is weakly supported.

Why it still matters. It is now the third independent instance of the same choice: szabo-2011-emotional-engine hand-authored every rule, belle-2019-programming-with-affect hand-scripted every trigger, and OCC recommends doing so. The corpus contains three votes for hand-coding and none for learning. This thesis is departing from unanimous practice, and the argument on this page has to be strong enough to carry that.

The departure is also narrower than the tally suggests, and worth stating precisely: all three hand-code the appraisal step (event → emotion), which is Q17. This page argues for learning the behaviour step (state → action). Those are separable, and the thesis could reasonably learn the second while deriving the first structurally — see appraisal-theory, where Gratch and Marsella propose a third option that is neither hand-authoring nor learning: reading appraisal variables off planning representations the agent maintains anyway.

That reading makes the corpus much less hostile than a raw count implies. The strongest version of the thesis' position: hand-authoring appraisal rules does not scale and nobody defends it on evidence; learning the policy is supported by sutton-barto-2018-rl and by social-competence's own requirement of responsiveness to training; and the appraisal layer can be derived rather than authored or learnt.

There is one further datapoint on the other side, already recorded on belle-2019-programming-with-affect: EMoBeT's authors explicitly design for emotional behaviour "regardless of whether it is an optimal outcome", against approaches that seek optimal responses. Under RL this thesis is optimising. Whether an optimised social robot is a legible one remains open — and it is not answered by any source in the corpus.

Not every observation is a sample — 2026-07-30

Ostension changes the update rule, not the attention weight

The page as written above treats every observation the same way: the agent acts, the world responds, the estimate moves by α. That is the correct default and it is what sutton-barto-2018-rl formalises.

topal-2008-perseverative and okumura-2020-ostension-attention show it is not what the model species does. When a demonstration is ostensively framed, infants encode a generalisable claim about the kind rather than an episodic fact about the token — from one or a few exposures, into a persisting trace, with a primacy rather than a recency profile. Strip the framing and leave the motor and visual experience identical, and they encode the episode instead (topal-2008-perseverative: perseverative error 0.81 → 0.48).

And it is specifically not an attention effect: matched attention-grabbing cues produce the orienting and none of the learning (okumura-2020-ostension-attention, Exp 3–4).

So there is a second place where the ethology belongs in the architecture. This page argues the repertoire belongs in A(s). The pedagogical stance says the learning rule itself is gated by a perceived social signal — one update mode for ostensively framed experience, another for everything else. Nothing in the RL corpus has a slot for that, and nothing in the ethorobotics corpus attempts it.

The mapping, its speculative status, and the acceptance test for the gate are on pedagogical-stance. Q20 asks whether a dog-modelled robot is entitled to the stance at all, since topal-2014-ostensive says dogs do not have it.

Three consequences for the design already on this page:

  • Shaping was always ostensive and the page did not say so. How learning happens: shaping, not deployment above argues that shaping is the ethologically native training method and satisfies social-competence's "responsiveness to learning and training". True — but shaping is a human deliberately signalling to a learner, and the wiki has been assuming an ostension-blind learner paired with an ostension-using trainer. If the robot cannot tell training from ambient events, every reward is an anonymous sample and the trainer's intent is discarded at the sensor.
  • Q14 gains a second axis. The open question was what collapses the emotion vector to a scalar. Add: a reward also carries an interpretive status. A treat given silently is one draw of R; a treat given with eye contact and dog-directed speech is a statement about the policy. The reward hypothesis (§3.2) has one slot and needs two. See reward-design.
  • T_safe acquires a specific job. An agent that privileges ostensively delivered instruction over its own perception is, by construction, manipulable — the documented failure mode in both infants and dogs (pedagogical-stance). The hand-authored safety subtree from colledanchise-ogren-behavior-trees §8.2 is where that bound should live, and it is a better-motivated use of it than the generic collision case.
