Verbal and visual cues that mark communicative intent — signals that say "this is addressed to you" before the content arrives.

Was the thinnest page in the wiki relative to its importance, resting on two unresolved references. Both are now held: topal-2014-ostensive ([3]) and albuquerque-2016-emotion-recognition ([4]).

What an ostensive signal is

A cue that gives evidence of the communicator's intention to convey information — separate from the information itself. From Sperber & Wilson's Relevance Theory and Csibra & Gergely's Natural Pedagogy, as presented in topal-2014-ostensive.

The key claim: ostensive cues do not work by grabbing attention. If they did, "any loud noise" would serve equally well. They work because the recipient is disposed to read them as a precursor to communication addressed to oneself.

Two intentions are distinguished and can be recognised separately: the communicative intention (that I am being addressed) and the informative intention (what I am being told).

What dogs do with them

topal-2014-ostensive's own four-point summary:

  1. Dogs treat ostensive signals as indicating that they are being instructed, not as mere discriminative cues — and this is "often indispensable" to their responding to referential gestures such as pointing or gaze shift.
  2. Eye contact is the strongest ostensive signal. High-pitched dog-directed speech ("doggerel", the analogue of motherese) is secondary but important. Name-calling is of minor importance — dogs respond the same to their own name and to an unfamiliar one (Kaminski et al. 2012).
  3. Ostensively cued behaviour "can often act as imperatives", inducing a "ready-to-obey" attitude. Hand-reared wolves show no comparable responsiveness.
  4. Non-ostensive attention-getters also affect responses, but dogs' specific responsiveness rests on perceiving communicative intention.

Eye contact is ambivalent, not simply positive. Between dogs it signals dominance and ritualised aggression; with an unfamiliar human in a threatening approach it evokes fear and raises heart rate. The communicative reading is an interspecific special case — an infant-like predisposition overriding a wolf-like aversion.

The limit, and the reframing that matters most

Dogs meet two of three criteria for being a recipient in pedagogical knowledge transfer. They are sensitive to ostension and understand referential cueing, but ostensive signals do not produce the infant's expectation of generic information. In dogs the attitude is "ready-to-learn or ready-to-obey" and "largely tied to the here and now".

So the authors reconceive the adaptive function:

not (only) knowledge acquisition from human partners but as facilitating behavioural synchronisation in order to avoid conflicts and/or to co-act in terms of common actions without necessarily comprehending the causal structure of the collaborative interaction.

No mind-reading is required — dogs "grasp the explicit or overt dimension of communication without any sophisticated mind-reading abilities", and "we are not necessarily entitled to call this phenomenon 'recognition' of intention".

A build specification, and a cheap one

topal-2014-ostensive states the minimum requirements for socially competent responding in ostensive situations: the ability to monitor, gain, maintain and shift attention while interacting with a human, and to share a common focus with them.

Five capacities, all about attention, none requiring a theory of mind — and every one implementable from what mecanumbot-perception already publishes (person pose, bearing, orientation) plus the neck for orienting and the LEDs for signalling attention state.

This is the clearest route in the corpus from ethology to a build spec, and it lowers the target: behavioural synchronisation rather than information transfer. Better evidenced, and it is what mecanumbot-behaviours' leading trees already attempt — get and hold a person's attention, share a focus.

This supersedes the previous version of this page, which said inbound ostensive detection "would have to be proxemic since gaze and speech are unavailable". The platform has a camera and 17-keypoint pose, so head orientation is available, and the audio path is unconfirmed rather than absent (robot-platform).

The failure mode this imports

Ostensive cueing overrides the dog's own evidence

Dogs follow deceptive pointing despite having seen the reward hidden elsewhere; select an empty container despite having watched the correct one baited; adopt inefficient object-manipulation strategies after demonstration; and abandon their preference for more food when a human ostensively prefers less (topal-2014-ostensive §11.3.1.3, citing Szetei 2003, Erdőhegyi 2007, Kupán 2011, Prato-Previde 2008). Human children show the same bias; dogs can learn to override it.

A robot built to be dog-like and ostension-responsive inherits this: an ostensively delivered instruction outweighs the robot's own perception. Safety-relevant for real-environment operation, and it should be a designed decision rather than an emergent one.

Read the other way, this is the same mechanism that makes shaping work (reward-design, conditioning). Trainability and manipulability are the same property — worth saying plainly in the thesis.

Inbound or outbound?

The corpus treats dogs as recipients. For emotion-engine the interesting direction may be the reverse — the robot producing ostensive signals so a person knows they are being addressed before the robot expresses anything.

Eye contact is the strongest such signal in dogs and robot-platform has no eyes, but it now has a neck, and orienting is the functional analogue. The nearest implemented precedent is the interaction-initiation component of the hearing-dog leading design (lakatos-2014-embodiments-demo).

Gating expression on ostension — so the robot responds to events addressed to it rather than to all detected affect — remains a design proposal of this wiki's. No source in the corpus tests robot-produced ostensive signalling. [unsourced] #needs-source
