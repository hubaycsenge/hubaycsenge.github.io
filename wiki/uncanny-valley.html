Mori's 1970 hypothesis: affinity toward an agent rises with human likeness, then collapses in a narrow band of very-close-but-imperfect similarity, before recovering at perfect humanness miklosi-2017-ethorobotics.

The dependent variable (Japanese shinwakan, "affinity") has two maxima — the Medium Peak at roughly 60–75% similarity, and the Maximum Peak at (near-)perfect similarity — with the valley between them.

Prior explanations divide into evolutionary (threat/death avoidance), developmental (appears in infants only after ~12 months), and perceptual/mental (competing mental representations). All assume the effect is specific to humans and to artificial creatures.

The ethorobotics reinterpretation

miklosi-2017-ethorobotics argues the effect is a special case of a general biological requirement: agents must categorise others accurately — conspecific vs heterospecific, familiar vs unfamiliar, individual vs group member — because the category determines what actions to take and what responses to expect.

On that reading, Mori's x-axis is really the conspecific/heterospecific boundary:

  • Medium Peak = interaction with heterospecifics sharing many human attributes — e.g. domesticated animals.
  • Maximum Peak = interaction with conspecifics.
  • Conspecific recognition is far more selective (mismating with hybrids or close relatives is costly), which is why the Maximum Peak has a narrower base.

The paradox, and why it matters here

Passing the valley solves nothing. Technology will not stop at human parity — a robot can have 360° vision where a human has gaze-following. So "trans-human" and then "post-human" robots fall into a second uncanny valley on the far side. The landscape is symmetric; humans avoid both the less and the overly human-like miklosi-2017-ethorobotics.

Therefore: go around the peak, not over it. Occupy a different niche defined by function (function-orientedness). This is the argument that makes ethorobotics a position rather than a preference, and it is why the dog model follows (dog-human-interaction-model).

Status of the evidence

The Hungarian engineering papers treat the valley cautiously and consistently: "although many take this effect for granted, only little actual research was devoted to this issue", and for 3D robots "the answer is presently less clear" (kovacs-fri-automaton-series, vincze-2012-interfaces-and-virca).

Worth keeping in view: the whole ethorobotics case rests partly on a hypothesis its own proponents describe as under-evidenced. Mathur & Reichling (2016) and Ferrey et al. (2015) are cited but not in the corpus — see citation-backlog.
