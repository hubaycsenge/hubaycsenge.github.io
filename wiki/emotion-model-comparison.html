Recommendation after reading both primaries — awaiting sign-off

Written 2026-07-28 after ingesting panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness, panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits and plutchik-1982-psychoevolutionary. Filed as a recommendation, not as a decision — Q1 remains open until Csenge confirms.

Keep the hybrid, but relabel which model supplies what:

Layer Source Why
Architecture Panksepp Parallel independent circuits; the three-level primary/secondary/tertiary split; the six-attribute system specification, including emotions outlast their stimuli — the temporal layer Szabó hand-built; and the homeostatic vs emotional affect distinction, which is the best answer Q2 has
State set + behaviour mapping Plutchik The functional language (fear→Protection, anger→Destruction, …) is function-orientedness arriving from emotion theory; the behavioural column maps onto motion primitives a faceless robot has; intensity is native; and it contains surprise, which Szabó implemented and Panksepp has no circuit for

Why this and not one or the other. The two models are not rival descriptions of one thing — Panksepp derives seven states from neural circuits identified by brain stimulation, Plutchik derives eight from functional behaviour prototypes across phylogeny. Different evidence, different lists, and neither refutes the other. Panksepp is strong exactly where Plutchik is empty (mechanism, neural grounding, dynamics, internal state) and empty exactly where Plutchik is strong (behavioural expression, function, intensity, and a state set implementable on a robot without a face).

This is also what the field already did, unacknowledged. Szabó's set is happiness, despair, fear, anger, surprise — surprise is Plutchik's, not Panksepp's. The corpus' "Panksepp-grounded" engine is already partly Plutchik-shaped. Making that explicit converts an anomaly into a design.

The cost, stated plainly: two models with incompatible ontologies are being stapled together, and neither author would endorse it. Panksepp insists on not mixing levels of analysis; Plutchik has no neural commitments to mix. The thesis must argue the hybrid, not assume it.

What would change the recommendation: if the engine's states must be defensible to an ethologist circuit-by-circuit, go pure Panksepp and accept dropping surprise. If the priority is legible expression on a faceless robot, go further toward Plutchik and treat the neural grounding as motivation rather than as structure.

A third model arrives — and it is not on the same axis

Added 2026-07-28 after ingesting ortony-2022-cognitive-structure. The recommendation above is deliberately left untouched; Csenge's steer was to record OCC and decide later.

occ-model is now held as a primary, and it is the model affective computing actually uses (belle-2019-programming-with-affect, GAMYGDALA, ALMA). The obvious move would be to reopen Q1 as a three-way choice. That would be a mistake, because the three models do not answer the same question.

Answers what the states are Answers how a situation produces one Answers what the state then does
panksepp-affective-systems ✅ 7 circuits partly — broad activators only partly — approach/avoid
plutchik-model ✅ 8 prototypes ✗ thin ✅ behavioural column
occ-model ✅ 24 types ✅ the only one that does ✗ nothing at all

OCC has no behavioural output. It differentiates 24 emotion types and says nothing about what an agent should do in any of them — no action tendency, no expression, no behavioural column. It is a theory of the front end.

Panksepp and Plutchik are the reverse: strong on states and (for Plutchik) on behaviour, thin on the path from event to state. Szabó's implemented hybrid inherits that weakness — its inputs were hand-labelled events, which is precisely the appraisal step OCC formalises.

Q1 has been the wrong shape

The wiki has framed Q1 as which model grounds the engine, and answered it with a state set. But an engine needs at least three things — an appraisal front end, a state representation, and a behaviour mapping — and no single model in the corpus supplies all three. The existing Panksepp+Plutchik recommendation covers the second and third. It says nothing about the first, and the first is where every implementation in the corpus resorted to hand-labelling.

This does not overturn the recommendation. It suggests Q1 should be split, and that the appraisal layer is a separate decision that has not yet been taken. Filed as a proposal in open-questions, not as a change to the recommendation.

The live option, then, is not "OCC instead of" but OCC as the appraisal front end feeding a Panksepp/Plutchik state set — with the caveat that mapping 24 OCC types onto 5–8 states is itself an unsolved design problem, and that OCC's own value system (goals, standards, tastes) would have to be represented explicitly on robot-platform. See appraisal-theory for the route Gratch and Marsella propose, which avoids hand-authoring the appraisal rules.

Two further considerations for whenever Q1 is settled:

  • OCC rejects dimensional representation outright — emotions "cannot be arranged informatively into any single space of reasonably low dimensionality". If the engine goes continuous for LED expression (the argument recorded below), that is a point against OCC and in Plutchik's favour.
  • OCC denies robots can have emotions at all (p. 219). Compatible with a functional engine, but it needs saying out loud — see machine-emotion-question.

The finding that reframes the question

szabo-2011-emotional-engine did not pick between an emotion theory and dog ethology. It used Panksepp as architectural justification for a state set derived from dog behaviour:

  • From Panksepp: several parallel, equally-ranked, independently-varying basic states, grounded in distinct neural circuits.
  • From dog ethology: which states — happiness, despair, fear, anger, surprise — and how environmental events move them.

Only FEAR and RAGE map onto Panksepp's circuits; SEEKING, LUST, CARE and PLAY have no counterpart in the implementation, and surprise has no Panksepp circuit at all.

So option 3 exists and is what the field actually did. The real question is narrower: what supplies the dynamics — intensity, blending, decay — that neither the circuit inventory nor the ethological table provides?

Recommendation, held loosely

Adopt the Szabó structure and extend it, rather than re-litigating the model choice. Reasons:

  1. It is the lineage's model, with an implementation, tables and arbitration rules to inherit (emotion-arbitration).
  2. The genuinely open problems — sensor-derived input, robot internal state, real-time autonomous operation — are orthogonal to the model choice. Time spent choosing between Panksepp and Plutchik is time not spent on the actual contribution (emotion-engine).
  3. Its known weaknesses are addressable within it: recover the long-term layer from szabo-2010-emotional-engine for temperament, and integrate the medium-term layer into arbitration, which Szabó explicitly left undone.

The argument on the other side, which should be recorded rather than dismissed: LED colour on robot-platform is a continuous expressive channel, and a continuous emotion space maps to it more naturally than five discrete states do. If expression is going to be primarily light-based, Plutchik's intensity and blending stop being a nicety.
