Read these across studies with care

Different emotion sets, different numbers of options (chance = 0.2 in Gácsi's five-way choice, 0.125 in Lakatos' eight-way), different presentation (video vs live interaction). The Kismet/Probo/Eddie/Feelix figures are reported second-hand by the Hungarian papers, not measured by them. Do not build a statistical comparison on this table; use it to establish orders of magnitude.

What the numbers actually support

1. Dog-derived body movement matches a human-like face. MogiRobi's 83.3% joy vs Kismet's 82.4%; PeopleBot's 75% overall vs Kismet's and Probo's 73%. Achieved without a face, without speech, and without adding non-functional hardware. This is the empirical core of the function-orientedness argument — legibility without imitation.

2. Robot ≈ dog. PeopleBot 75% vs the live dog's 78%, with no significant difference overall in the model (p = 0.16). The ceiling is not far above where these robots already are.

3. Fear is the universal failure. ~47–48% for both MogiRobi and Kismet; 65% for Probo. Consistent across face-based and body-based expression, and dog owners are bad at recognising fear in real dogs from visual signals alone. Robot fear is typically misread as indifference, passivity or reluctance — and in lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution participants responded to it with anger and embarrassment.

Design consequence

If emotion-engine adopts Szabó's arbitration rule that fear above medium intensity overrides everything (emotion-arbitration), it will preferentially express the one state humans reliably fail to read — and misread in a way that produces negative user reactions. The priority rule is biologically motivated but may be pragmatically wrong for HRI. Q12 in open-questions.

4. Nobody has measured this on a light-and-motion-only robot. Every figure above comes from a robot with either a face or articulated appendages. robot-platform has neither. There is no baseline for what is achievable with omnidirectional motion plus four RGB LEDs — which makes it a genuine contribution to establish one.
