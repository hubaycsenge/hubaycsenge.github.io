The perception front-end of emotion-engine — and, after ingesting the corpus, the largest hole in both the literature and the platform.

The state of things

No source in the corpus does this

Every emotion model here takes hand-labelled discrete events as input. szabo-2011-emotional-engine and szabo-2010-emotional-engine feed the engine from a MATLAB GUI. kovacs-fri-automaton-series feeds the automaton geometric inputs (distances, presence flags). The empirical studies (lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution, gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution) were teleoperated. The perception-to-emotion link has never been closed in this lineage.

References [5] and [6] of research-proposal — the sensor-based emotion analysis citations — are not in the corpus at all. This is the biggest coverage gap. See citation-backlog.

Of the surveyed robots, only two do any affect recognition: Ifbot recognises emotion from voice tone via a five-layer perceptron; EmotiRob takes video and audio and associates each action or word with a vector of primary emotions (prior-robot-platforms). Neither is described in enough detail to reuse.

The platform constraint has changed — 2026-07-28

There is a camera, and the input is body pose

This section previously argued from "robot-platform documents no camera and no microphone". That was wrong, and the re-clipped repositories (mecanumbot-repos) correct it. The platform has a CSI camera and a working detection pipeline (mecanumbot-perception) publishing the 17 COCO pose keypoints — nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles — fused with lidar into people in map space.

The real input set is therefore neither lidar proxemics nor facial expression, but the thing between them: posture, orientation, gesture, gait, and spatial arrangement between people. For a thesis arguing function-orientedness, that is a better position than either option this page was choosing between — it is much closer to what a dog reads off a human, and it keeps the Q5 objection answered.

What has not changed is the literature gap. No node infers affect; no source in the corpus maps body pose to emotional state; references [5] and [6] of research-proposal are still missing. The platform can now see. Nothing tells it what it is looking at.

The proxemic and kinematic set below remains valid and is now a subset of what is available, not the whole of it. It is still what the attachment-sst-derived rule base uses, and it is still the cheapest route:

  • proxemics — distance, and change in distance
  • approach and retreat speed
  • orientation and dwell time
  • movement history — as ichikawa-numakunai-2012-roman models for the robot, applied to the human
  • presence and number of people, familiar vs unfamiliar if identity is available

This may be a better fit, not a worse one

These are exactly the inputs the attachment-sst-derived rules already use: OwnerToDogDistance, StrangerToDogDistance, OwnerInTheRoom, ThePlaceIsUnknown. A lidar-only pipeline is directly compatible with the existing ethological rule base, needs no training data, and is cheap enough to support the real-time claim.

It is also more consistent with function-orientedness: facial-expression recognition is the part of affective computing most committed to human-likeness (Q5). Reading a person's behaviour in space is what a dog does.

The cost is honesty about scope — this is affect inference from behaviour, not emotion recognition in the affective-computing sense. The thesis would need to say so plainly. That remains true of the keypoint route as well.

What to hunt for now

The search has become much more specific, and much more tractable. The wanted literature is bodily and postural affect recognition from skeletal keypoints — not facial expression, not speech. Terms that will find it: body pose affect recognition, bodily expression of emotion, skeleton-based emotion recognition, gait and affect, proxemics and interpersonal distance.

This is a real and reasonably mature subfield, and none of it is in the wiki. Getting two or three papers here would close the single largest coverage gap in citation-backlog. Also worth checking whether any of it uses COCO 17-keypoint input specifically, since that would be directly reusable on mecanumbot-perception's output.

Still to confirm: whether mecanumbot_audio is capture or playback. If capture, vocal prosody becomes available and Ifbot's approach (voice tone → perceptron) becomes a comparable precedent. #needs-source

The proposal's own references point at face and voice, not body

Refs [5] and [6] are now identified (citation-backlog): Wani et al. (2021) on speech emotion recognition and Li & Deng (2022) on facial expression recognition. So the plan was face + voice.

robot-platform delivers body pose and an unconfirmed audio path. The literature the thesis actually needs — skeleton-based and bodily affect recognition — is not on the reference list at all. That gap is the single most valuable thing to close, and closing it is also what makes the function-orientedness argument survive: reading posture is what a dog does; reading faces is the part of affective computing most committed to human likeness (Q5).

And there is a ceiling. albuquerque-2016-emotion-recognition shows dogs recognise emotion cross-modally in both dogs and humans — but more than twice as strongly for conspecifics (congruence index 0.26 vs 0.12). A faithfully dog-modelled recogniser should be expected to be worse at humans than at its own kind. Worth stating before a reviewer does.

Assessment axes for anything ingested here

  • Modalities; early or late fusion
  • Latency and compute footprint — decisive for Q3
  • Validation setting: lab, staged, or in the wild
  • Training data and population — generalisation to real users is a known weakness [unsourced] #needs-source
  • Whether it presupposes a human-like agent (Q5)
  • Output format — must match whatever emotion-model-comparison settles on

The gap may be mis-specified — 2026-07-28

This page has framed the problem as no source says how to read affect off a body pose. ortony-2022-cognitive-structure Ch. 10 argues the frame itself is wrong.

"Much of affective computing focuses on recognizing a person's emotions from surface cues alone, yet external expressions have only a loose connection to internal states. Rather, observers are more accurate at forming affective inferences when they bring to bear knowledge such as information about the immediately preceding events." (Gratch & Marsella, p. 242, citing L. F. Barrett et al. and Ortony & Turner — both unheld, see citation-backlog)

Their proposed remedy is appraisal-theory: if a system knows a person's goals and can derive appraisals from the situation, it can predict how they would feel — and appraisal judgements, they report, "explain much of the variance of people's self-reported feelings" better than surface characteristics do.

Contradiction — with this page's own working assumption

The wiki has assumed the perception gap closes with a better classifier over richer body-pose data, and that mecanumbot-perception's 17-keypoint pipeline put the platform most of the way there. Gratch and Marsella's claim is that no classifier over surface features closes it, because the signal is not there to be extracted.

Which is better supported? Their claim rests on citations this corpus does not hold, so it cannot be verified here. But it is consistent with a pattern the wiki has already recorded independently: recognition rates in emotion-recognition-benchmarks plateau well short of ceiling, and lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution found fear read correctly only 48% of the time — for a robot deliberately signalling it. If deliberate expression is read at 48%, incidental pose is unlikely to do better.

Not resolved. Recorded as Q19 in open-questions. Obtaining Barrett et al. is now the highest-value acquisition for this page.

If the claim holds, the consequences are architectural rather than incremental:

  • Pose becomes one input among several, not the input.
  • The robot's event and interaction history becomes load-bearing — it must know what just happened to the person, not only what they look like now.
  • The perception module stops being separable from the emotion engine, because appraisal needs the same world model the engine uses.
  • It changes what validation-study measures: recognition accuracy in isolation would be the wrong endpoint.

This does not make the platform's pose pipeline wasted — it is necessary, and it is the part that is built. It makes it insufficient.

Gaps

wiki/datasets/ is still empty — no corpus or benchmark is recorded, because no ingested source uses one. If the lidar route is taken, the relevant "dataset" may be recorded interaction sessions, not a public affect corpus. The 2026-07-28 reframing strengthens that guess: sessions carry the event history that isolated affect corpora strip out.
