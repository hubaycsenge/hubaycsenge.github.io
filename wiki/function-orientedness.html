An ethorobotics principle: start from function and environment, then design the best-suited agent, independent of similarity to humans miklosi-2017-ethorobotics.

The ethological root is Tinbergen's focus on the function of behaviour in relation to the environment in which the species evolved. Applied to robotics: the robot should have a function, and its embodiment, behaviour and cognitive abilities should fit its specific environment.

Two arguments for it beyond the uncanny-valley escape:

  • Robot engineering is not constrained by evolutionary continuity — it can jump between radically different designs, so there is no reason to inherit human form.
  • Humans may not be well designed for many tasks, so uncritical copying is wasted effort.

The operational test

Applied on every ingest: does this method require the robot to look or act human in order to work, or only to be legible? Legibility is compatible with function-orientedness; imitation is not.

The corpus makes the distinction concrete. gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution rejects adding arms to a robot purely for emotional display —

"it does not seem practical to add extra non-functional features, such as arms and hands... just for displaying some emotional expressions, because this would make the robot unnecessarily expensive."

— and notes that most social robots "do not need verbal abilities or a human-like face to fit their original function". lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution makes the same point: robots should be "functional with regard to their roles in the human community, and their social behaviour should mirror this function as well, taking into account the embodiment and the technological constraints".

Now supported empirically, not just argued

emotion-recognition-benchmarks is the evidence: dog-derived body movement on a faceless PeopleBot reached 75%, against Kismet's 73% with a purpose-built expressive face. MogiRobi's 83.3% joy against Kismet's 82.4%. The functional route is not a compromise — it performs equivalently, at lower hardware cost.

And lakatos-2014-embodiments-demo shows the same behavioural design working across four embodiments from a bare holonomic base to an 11-DOF expressive head.

Consequence for this thesis

robot-platform has no non-functional expressive hardware at all — its LEDs and mecanum drive both serve primary functions. That is maximally function-oriented, and makes the platform an unusually clean test of the principle. It also means every expressive behaviour must be built from motion quality and light, with no fallback. See Q4 in open-questions.

Tracked as Q5 for the risk in the other direction: affective computing methods imported wholesale carry anthropomorphic assumptions.
