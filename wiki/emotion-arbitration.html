The step between having an emotional state vector and doing something about it: which emotion, of several simultaneously active, gets expressed? The wiki did not have this concept before ingesting szabo-2011-emotional-engine; it is a required component of emotion-engine.

Two approaches in the corpus

Winner-take-all — Kismet's approach. The strongest dimension is expressed. Simple; produces abrupt switching and cannot express ambivalence.

Hierarchical rule tree — szabo-2011-emotional-engine, explicitly proposed against winner-take-all:

  1. Exactly one positive dimension → express it.
  2. Several positive dimensions: - a. fear > 3 (medium) → express fear, regardless of all others; - b. a strict maximum exists → express it; - c. tie → fixed hierarchy fear > surprise > anger > happiness = despair; - d. tie at ≥ 3 → express the top two as mixed or alternating behaviour.

A team of units under one broadcast signal — a third option, from sutton-barto-2018-rl §15.10 and actor-critic. Several behaviour units each receive the same scalar reinforcement signal δ, each explores independently, and the expressed behaviour is their collective output. No explicit selection rule at all. Williams (1992) showed such a team ascends the gradient of the shared reward without the units communicating.

Arbitration and credit assignment are the same question

§15.10 recasts winner-take-all not as a naïve default but as a deliberate simplification of structural credit assignment: "An extreme case is a winner-take-all arrangement (for example, the result of lateral inhibition in the brain) that restricts collective actions to those to which only one, or a few, team members contribute. In this case the winners get the credit or blame."

Once behaviour is learnt (rl-for-behaviour-learning), this matters concretely. A hierarchical rule tree gives no way to attribute an outcome back to the components that produced it, because the rule, not the outcome, decides. Winner-take-all does. The choice of arbitration scheme therefore constrains what can be learnt, which is an argument the corpus does not contain and which partly reopens the case for the option Szabó et al. rejected. (Inference from §15.10; the book discusses neither emotion nor arbitration.)

The biological justification, and its weakness

Grounded in MacLean's triune brain: fear and anger are ancestral ("reptilian"), happiness and despair belong to newer cortical regions associated with higher cognition and social contexts. Surprise sits between, as the startle reaction — whose function is to supply extra information to the nervous system and speed up decisions about an adaptive response.

The authors undercut their own justification

szabo-2011-emotional-engine states plainly that the triune-brain hypothesis "is obsolete in the light of newer neurobiological findings", then uses it anyway because it "can be valid and helpful in setting up the hierarchy". The arbitration hierarchy is therefore motivated by a superseded model. Any reuse in this thesis needs either a better justification or an empirical one (e.g. tune the hierarchy to what humans actually read correctly).

Mixed emotions — and what they are not

Rule 2d means ambivalent behaviour across modalities: the example given is an animal threatened by an approaching stranger that lowers itself submissively (fear) while emitting aggressive vocalisations. Or the same modality alternating rapidly, depending on the system's expressive capacity.

This is explicitly not Plutchik's secondary/tertiary dyads (plutchik-model) — the paper cites Plutchik only to disclaim the resemblance.

Open issues carried forward

  • The medium-term layer is not integrated. Arbitration uses only short-term output yR; the authors call integrating yM future work. So the robot's "mood" influences the state but never directly the decision. Unresolved.
  • The fear-override rule may be counterproductive in HRI. Fear is the least reliably recognised emotion across every robot measured (emotion-recognition-benchmarks) and is typically misread as indifference, provoking negative user reactions (lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution). A rule that prioritises it maximises exposure to the failure mode. Q12 in open-questions.
  • Mixed/alternating expression is untested. No study in the corpus measures whether humans can read a mixed emotional display at all.
  • On robot-platform, "different modalities" means motion vs LEDs — the natural place for ambivalent expression, and an untried one.
