First-party source. States the thesis, the theoretical commitments, and the claimed novelty. Everything currently in this wiki traces back here, which means the wiki is currently unvalidated against published literature — that is the first gap to close.

Takeaways

  • Positions the work inside ethorobotics: technology built on ethological findings, treating HRI as an interspecific interaction and committing to function-orientedness over human imitation.
  • Dogs as the model species for robot social behaviour, justified by their understanding of ostensive-signalling and their reading of human vocal and visual emotional expression.
  • Emotion attribution to artificial agents facilitates interaction — the premise licensing an affective layer at all (emotion-attribution).
  • The system must fuse two input streams: perceived human affect, and the robot's own internal state (battery, sensor and motor health). Fusing proprioceptive-style signals with social perception is the distinctive move here — most affective HRI work models only the human.
  • Two candidate grounding models named: panksepp-affective-systems and plutchik-model. The choice is left open — this is the live decision.
  • Implementation lineage: fuzzy-logic emotion engines, extensible with transfer learning; behaviour-trees for emotion-based decision-making.
  • Validation is on human subjects (validation-study).

Claimed novelty

Real-time event detection in real environments, as against lab-bound implementations. This is the claim the wiki should be hardest on: every ingested system needs assessing for where it was validated and at what latency.

Limitations of this source

  • Unpublished, unreviewed, and self-authored — it establishes intent, not fact.
  • References [1]–[10] are cited by number with no bibliography supplied. All ten are unresolved; see citation-backlog. Until they are resolved, most claims in this wiki rest on an uncheckable citation.
  • No robot platform is named (robot-platform is a stub awaiting this).
  • No dataset, evaluation metric, or success criterion is specified.
