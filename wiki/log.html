[2026-07-28] ingest | Sutton & Barto (2018), Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction

Full interactive ingest of raw/papers/BartoSutton.pdf, replacing the table-of-contents-only stub written during the 2026-07-27 batch. Read closely: Ch. 14 Psychology, Ch. 15 Neuroscience, §17.4 Designing Reward Signals, §17.5, plus §1.3–1.4, §3.1–3.2 and the Comments on Terminology. Parts I–II recorded structurally only.

Csenge's steer, mid-ingest: use reinforcement learning to teach the robot behaviour patterns. That closes Q11 and the ingest was written accordingly.

What the book turned out to be. Not a source on emotion. Verified against the full text: Panksepp appears zero times, emotion three times (all incidental), affective twice in the body. §14.7 explicitly disclaims links to ethology and behavioural ecology. What it is: the formal apparatus for the animal-learning phenomena the rest of the corpus models by hand.

Key findings

  • §14.3 — Thorndike's "instinctual impulses" are the admissible action set A(s), and "specifying these sets... can radically simplify learning". This answers the standing objection that RL trades away ethologist-validatable rules: keep the dog-derived primitives as A(s), learn the selection over them. The whole design argument in rl-for-behaviour-learning rests on this sentence.
  • social-competence already required this. miklosi-2017-ethorobotics lists "responsiveness to learning and training" among the dog-derived properties a socially competent robot needs, and no system in the corpus satisfies it. That is the in-corpus mandate for the RL decision, and it had been sitting unremarked on the page since 2026-07-27.
  • Reward is internal, and the agent is not the robot (§3.1, §14 terminology). Motors, sensors and the reward computation are all on the environment side. The emotional engine therefore sits outside the RL agent and computes its reward.
  • Reward ≠ reinforcement; the TD error δ, not R, drives learning (§15.2).
  • Actor–critic maps onto the striatum (§15.7), and §15.10's team formulation gives emotion-arbitration a third option beside winner-take-all and rule tree — plus the observation that arbitration and structural credit assignment are the same question.
  • Model-free/model-based ↔ habitual/goal-directed (§14.6), with overtraining converting one into the other (Adams 1982). Gives social-competence's untested "emerges over time" construct a mechanism and an instrument.

Corrections to the previous stub

  • The SEEKING–dopamine bridge is not supported by this book and the earlier page's speculative callout has been rewritten as a warning on panksepp-affective-systems. The book never mentions Panksepp; Ch. 15 is about dopamine and reward prediction, not about any named affective system.
  • The stub described the book as "reference only — role in the thesis unclear". Superseded by the steer above.

Recorded as a within-source inconsistency: the reward-modulated STDP eligibility window is given as "up to 10 seconds" in §15.8 and §15.13 but as "0.3 to 2 seconds" in the bibliographic note for §15.8, both citing Yagishita et al. (2014). Unresolvable from the book; nothing in the wiki depends on it.

Counter-evidence recorded, not suppressed: §17.5 states that current methods "struggle to learn rapidly in the incremental, online settings", that catastrophic interference is unsolved, and that safe embedding of RL agents in physical environments is "one of the most pressing areas for future research". This undercuts step 8 of thesis as an engineering claim while supporting it as a research target. Both readings are on the page.

On 2026-07-27 this wiki floated a bridge from Panksepp's SEEKING circuit to the TD reward-prediction error and flagged it [unsourced], pending a Panksepp primary. Recorded as a > [!warning] Contradiction on panksepp-affective-systems and td-learning, with both positions quoted and the asymmetry stated: Panksepp disputes that RPE exhausts SEEKING, not that SEEKING is dopaminergic; sutton-barto-2018-rl never discusses affect.

Panksepp — a second correction. "All primal emotional systems interact with each other in primal inhibitory and excitatory relationships that no one has yet mapped empirically." panksepp-affective-systems previously claimed inter-circuit inhibition gives a principled basis for arbitration rules. It does not. Second problem for emotion-arbitration, on top of the obsolete triune-brain justification.

Plutchik is stronger than the corpus made him look. The functional language — fear→Protection, anger→Destruction, anticipation→Exploration — is function-orientedness arriving from emotion theory in 1982, and the behavioural column maps onto motion primitives a faceless robot has. Also explains surprise, which Szabó implemented and Panksepp has no circuit for.

Q2 — three traditions converge. Panksepp splits homeostatic from emotional affects; Plutchik splits motivation from emotion; sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3 makes internal state a modulator of what is rewarding. All three say internal state is a different category, not another dimension of the affect vector. Best support Q2 has had.

[2026-07-28] ingest | Colledanchise & Ögren, Behavior Trees in Robotics and AI

Full 198-page version, replacing the 45-page preview clipped earlier the same day. Ingested on Csenge's steer: behaviour trees as the executive method for decisions made by reinforcement learning algorithms.

Provenance recorded: the PDF is arXiv:1709.00084v6, dated 25 Oct 2022, printed from a browser — not the CRC Press 2018 print edition, and four years newer. Cited by chapter and section, never by page.

Read closely: Ch. 1 (formulation, node semantics, applications), Ch. 2 (relation to FSMs, HFSMs, Subsumption, Teleo-Reactive, Decision Trees, and the advantages/disadvantages list), Ch. 3 (design principles), Ch. 8 (machine learning). Chapters 5, 6, 7 and 9 read for scope only — recorded by what they deliver, not derived. Flagged on the source page so no formal guarantee gets claimed from a chapter that was skimmed.

The finding that matters. §8.4:

"If RL-BT was started from scratch with a BT consisting of a single action, there would be no difference between RL-BT and standard RL. Instead, the point of combining BTs with RL is to address the curse of dimensionality, and do RL on smaller state spaces, while the BT connects these subproblems in a modular way."

So the BT is not downstream plumbing — it decomposes the learning problem. That is a stronger claim than the steer assumed, and it composes with sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3 (ethological repertoire as A(s)): the ethology supplies which primitives exist, the tree supplies how the learning is carved up between them. Neither source makes the other's point.

RL-BT mechanics recorded: a hand-designed BT with selected nodes replaced by Learning Nodes — a Learning Action node wrapping Q-learning over a local state/action/reward, and a Learning Control Flow node, a Fallback that learns the order to try its own children. Costs more designer input than plain RL, not less; for this thesis that is a feature.

Safety now has a mechanism. Sequence(T_safe, T_learn) — a hand-authored safety subtree that overrides the learned tree, with a conservative margin. This is a concrete answer to the problem sutton-barto-2018-rl §17.5 names and does not solve. Recorded on rl-for-behaviour-learning with the chattering failure mode.

Q9 settled — behaviour tree, with learning nodes. BTs generalise FSMs, HFSMs, Subsumption, Teleo-Reactive programs and Decision Trees, so nothing expressible in fuzzy-automaton-fri is lost by structure. And §8.2.4.1 measured an FSM-based learner's node count growing exponentially while the BT's growth rate declined; §2.6 gives the reason — FSM transitions are one-way control transfers, Dijkstra's goto. A fuzzy automaton is a state machine, so its transition function is both what learning displaces and the part that scales badly under it.

The residue of Q9, kept explicit: the automaton does behaviour fusion (continuous blending by necessity level — "somewhat anxious while still exploring"); a BT selects. That loss is real and is not resolved by this source.

Q3 gains an analytic tool. Ch. 9 converts Stochastic BTs into Discrete Time Markov Chains, yielding Mean Time To Succeed, Mean Time To Fail and success probability over time, composable across subtrees — a defensible answer to "will it complete in time and how often will it fail" that does not require running the robot. Pairs with the measured end-to-end latency already proposed.

Coverage gap only partly closed, and this matters. The book has no emotion content whatever, and its only social-robotics entry is a single paragraph on the JIBO SDK. The wiki's "behaviour trees in HRI — nothing" gap survives. Belle, Gittens & Graham (2019), Programming with Affect (IEEE GEM 2019) is now the single most important unheld source: it is the only item in the thesis reference list joining behaviour trees to emotion, and the nearest prior art to the decision layer.

Disadvantages recorded from §2.6.2 rather than glossed: the engine is complex to implement; checking all conditions can be expensive (relevant — the platform now runs two neural detectors plus fusion); feed-forward execution is fine in structured environments; BTs need a different mindset (condition-driven and tick-driven, not state- and event-driven); tooling is less mature than for FSMs.

[2026-07-28] ingest | Belle, Gittens & Graham (2019), Programming with Affect

The last unheld numeric reference ([9]/[10]), and the one flagged in the preceding entry as most important. IEEE GEM 2019, 8 pages, downloaded via ELTE from IEEE Xplore. All ten numeric references of research-proposal are now identified and eight are held; only [5] and [6] remain outstanding.

What it is. EMoBeT — a behaviour-tree extension making mood a first-class property, via two node types: an Emotion Adder (fires a developer-specified OCC emotion from a hard-wired trigger) and an E-Selector (chooses between exactly two children by the sign of the character's mood). Built on a light-weight reimplementation of Gebhard's ALMA, with OCC emotions, a PAD mood space of eight octants, Five Factor personality setting default mood and scaling emotion intensity, and a memory module decaying on the Ebbinghaus curve that re-triggers emotions when a previously-met player returns.

The finding that matters most. Every emotion in every scenario is fired by a scripted player dialogue choice with a hard-wired trigger — hand-labelled discrete events, exactly as in szabo-2011-emotional-engine's MATLAB GUI.

Two entirely independent traditions — Hungarian ethorobotics, and Caribbean/Canadian game AI using OCC and PAD instead of Panksepp and dog ethology — arrive at the same three-layer architecture and stop at the same place. Neither closes the perception-to-emotion link, and neither cites the other. Recorded on thesis: the gap is field-wide, not lineage-specific, which materially strengthens the contribution claim.

Novelty assessment, stated precisely. EMoBeT does pre-empt "behaviour-tree nodes that make affective state first-class in action selection" — that idea is prior art as of 2019 and the thesis cannot claim it. It does not have a robot, sensing of any kind, learning, agent internal state, a real environment, or any evaluation: §V is titled "Scenarios and Evaluation" but contains only worked traces of PAD values through three scripted dialogues, and the conclusion defers usability and performance testing — "to determine if it is a viable approach" — to future work. The idea is prior art; the evidence is not.

A live design question now has prior art to differ from. behaviour-trees asked whether emotional state should gate the tree, re-weight it, or parameterise leaf actions. Belle et al. gate, and collapse an eight-octant PAD mood to one bit at the decision point. The wiki's existing argument for parameterising leaf actions stands and is sharper for having an alternative: approach appears under joy, fear, neutral and anger in dog-human-interaction-model, distinguished by manner, and robot-platform's channels (LED colour, speed, proximity, neck angle) are continuous.

Two things EMoBeT has that the Hungarian lineage lacks, recorded on emotion-engine: episodic memory of specific individuals with decay — which is what a longitudinal test of social-competence would need (Q16) — and a retained long-term/personality layer, where Szabó's long-term layer appears in 2010 and is gone by 2012.

Q5 gains a clear datapoint. EMoBeT is built entirely from human psychology — OCC, PAD, Five Factor, human dialogue — and none of it would survive translation to a faceless wheeled robot. Their stated stance, simulating human-like emotion "regardless of whether it is an optimal outcome", is also the opposite of what rl-for-behaviour-learning proposes; that tension needs an answer in the thesis.

[2026-07-28] ingest | Ortony, Clore & Collins — The Cognitive Structure of Emotions (2nd edn, 2022)

Source: Ortony, Clore & Collins, The Cognitive Structure of Emotions, 2nd edn, Cambridge University Press 2022 (313 pp.), in raw/papers/ → ortony-2022-cognitive-structure

Interactive ingest. Discussed before writing; Csenge steered two decisions — record OCC and leave the Q1 recommendation untouched, and escalate the contradictions to a dedicated synthesis page rather than only annotating affected pages.

The OCC primary, held at last. citation-backlog item 2 listed it as the third emotion model in the corpus and the one game AI actually uses, reached until now only second-hand through belle-2019-programming-with-affect.

It is the 2022 second edition, not the 1988 original, and the difference matters: 22 → 24 emotion types; "eliciting conditions" replaced by emergence conditions (a deliberate move toward constructivism); intensity substantially expanded; and a new contributed chapter by Gratch and Marsella on OCC and affective computing. Belle et al. cite the 1988 edition, so their emotion set cannot be assumed identical.

Four new pages

  • occ-model — 24 types in six groups; desirability/praiseworthiness/appeal; global and local intensity variables; the two-stage potential→threshold formalism.
  • appraisal-theory — the tradition; appraisal variables as by-products of competent agency; the appraisal→coping→reappraisal loop; reverse appraisal.

What moved

  • A stage the wiki had never named. The engine runs events → state → expression, with no account of how an event becomes an appraised event. Every implementation in the corpus hand-labelled it — Szabó via a MATLAB GUI, Belle et al. via scripted dialogue. That stage is appraisal, and OCC is the only model held that formalises it. New Q17, arguably sharper than Q1.
  • Q1 proposed to split. Not reopened — the Panksepp+Plutchik recommendation stands per Csenge's steer. But an engine needs an appraisal front end, a state set and a behaviour mapping, and no single model supplies all three: OCC has 24 types and no behavioural output at all; Panksepp and Plutchik are thin on the trigger side. Proposed as Q1a/Q1b on emotion-model-comparison.
  • emotion-attribution gains a criterion, from outside HRI. OCC's Attraction emotions split on whether the object is construed as emotion-capable, and the criterion is responsiveness and apparent reciprocity — gaze exchange, contingent reaction to small movements — explicitly not resemblance or animacy. The worked cases are a dog versus a pet skunk, and domestic robots are named (p. 180). This closes the page's own open item ("morphology, contingency, or both?") in favour of contingency, and it is buildable and manipulable on robot-platform.
  • function-orientedness corroborated from the tradition Q5 warns about.
  • emotion-arbitration gains two mechanisms: per-person, per-time emotion thresholds — which make competing emotions commensurable and give Q12 a better answer than a fear-override rule — and EMA's control-based coping selection.
  • The perception gap may be mis-specified. New Q19. Gratch and Marsella: "external expressions have only a loose connection to internal states"; accuracy needs preceding events and the person's goals (p. 242). If right, no classifier over pose closes the gap, and the robot's interaction history becomes load-bearing.

Two contradictions recorded

Both from Ch. 9 §Computational Tractability, p. 219, in the authors' own voice.

  1. OCC denies robots can have emotions — "not the kinds of things that can be conscious". Narrower than it reads: it denies experience, not function, and the same paragraph affirms emotion's relevance to robotics. machine-emotion-question argues the thesis should adopt functional language explicitly and the objection dissolves. Q18. Opposing camp — Sloman & Croucher (1981) — unheld.
  2. OCC prefers hand-coding to learning. Asserted without evidence, and about the appraisal layer rather than the policy — but it is now the third independent vote for hand-coding, against none for learning. rl-for-behaviour-learning argues the departure is defensible and narrower than the tally implies.
