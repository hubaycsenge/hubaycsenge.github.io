Jaak Panksepp, Affective consciousness: Core emotional feelings in animals and humans, Consciousness and Cognition 14(1):30–80, March 2005. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2004.10.004

Reference [8] of research-proposal, now held. The wiki carried Panksepp second-hand via szabo-2011-emotional-engine for a week; this is the primary, and it is the fuller theoretical statement. Its companion, panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits, is the shorter and later summary.

Takeaways

  • The seven systems, first-hand: SEEKING, FEAR, RAGE, LUST, CARE, PANIC, PLAY. Confirms the list the wiki had via Szabó. Described as "the neurodynamic attractor landscapes of a variety of extended trans-diencephalic, limbic emotional action systems".
  • A terminological hierarchy the wiki has been missing. Emotion is the umbrella term covering "affective, cognitive, behavioral, expressive, and a host of physiological changes". Affect is "the subjective experiential-feeling component". And affects divide into emotional (linked to internal brain action states, triggered by environmental events), homeostatic (bodily — hunger, thirst) and sensory (taste, touch). See Q2 below.
  • Three psychological dimensions are acknowledged: valence, arousal, and "surgency or power (how much does a certain feeling fill one's mental life)". Panksepp is not a purely discrete theorist — he names a dimensional description and then argues the causal structure is circuit-based.
  • "There is currently no agreed upon taxonomy of affective states." Worth quoting when the thesis has to justify picking one.
  • Six defining characteristics of an emotional system (Fig. 1): unconditional sensory access; instinctual motor outputs; modulation of sensory inputs (incentive salience); positive feedback components that sustain arousal after the precipitating event has passed; modulation by cognitive inputs; and the capacity to modify and channel cognitive activity.
  • Primary / secondary / tertiary consciousness. Primary = raw sensory/perceptual feelings and internal emotional experience; secondary = thoughts about experiences; tertiary = thoughts about thoughts, largely human and language-dependent.
  • Dual-aspect monism (Spinozan): emotional feelings reflect the neurodynamics of the brain systems that generate instinctual emotional behaviour. This is the methodological premise that licenses reading affect off behaviour — the same move ethorobotics makes when it reads emotion off robot movement.
  • The capitalisation convention is argued, not stylistic: capitals designate specific neural systems, and guard against "part–whole confusions" — the error of assuming a circuit explains everything the vernacular word connotes.

The RPE contradiction, now doubly and directly sourced

§7 of this paper is titled:

The SEEKING/expectancy/wanting system of the brain: It's not just "reward prediction error"

and the body states that emotional systems are "unlikely that they can be encapsulated by the concept of reinforcement or even more subtle variants such as 'reward prediction errors' (Schultz, 1998, 2000, 2002)".

Contradiction — Panksepp vs Sutton & Barto

sutton-barto-2018-rl (§15.3–15.6) advances the reward prediction error hypothesis: the phasic activity of dopamine neurons signals a TD error. The evidence is Schultz's monkey experiments, and the book calls the correspondence "remarkably resilient".

Panksepp (2005 §7, and again in panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits) rejects the reduction: treating SEEKING as an RPE signal is "mixing levels of analysis", and the dopamine system was "long misconceptualized as 'The Brain Reward, Pleasure and/or Reinforcement System'".

What is actually in dispute. Panksepp does not deny that SEEKING is dopaminergic — he calls it "this dopamine facilitated MFB circuitry". Nor does he deny that RPE models fit Schultz's data. He denies that RPE exhausts what the system is: for him it is a primary-process affective system with a felt character, of which reward prediction is one secondary-process function. Sutton & Barto are describing a learning signal; Panksepp is describing an affective system that among other things supports learning.

Which is better supported depends on the question. For "what does phasic dopamine activity compute", the RPE hypothesis has the stronger and more quantitative evidence. For "what is the SEEKING system for", Panksepp's level objection stands, and Sutton & Barto do not contest it — they never discuss affect at all. The two are not symmetrical rivals, and the wiki should not present them as such.

Consequence. The SEEKING↔TD-error bridge that this wiki floated on 2026-07-27 and flagged as [unsourced] is now explicitly rejected by its own putative source. It must not appear in the thesis in that form. See panksepp-affective-systems and td-learning.

What this changes for Q2

The emotional / homeostatic / sensory split is the most directly useful thing here. open-questions Q2 has been asking how battery charge is commensurable with human affect. Panksepp's answer is that it is not the same kind of affect — homeostatic affects arise from interoceptors and are a distinct category that coexists with emotional affects in one architecture.

For emotion-engine that reframes the design: internal state is not another dimension of the emotional vector, it is a parallel affect channel that modulates what the emotional systems do. That is close to the reward-modulation route reward-design draws from sutton-barto-2018-rl §14.3 — hunger changing what food is worth. Two independent sources converging on the same architecture is the strongest support Q2 has.

Method and limitations

A theoretical review essay in a consciousness-studies journal, not an empirical report. Its evidence base is prior work — electrical (ESB) and chemical brain stimulation, self-stimulation, conditioned place preference and aversion — overwhelmingly in rats, and much of it Panksepp's own.

  • Polemical. Substantial parts argue against named opponents (LeDoux, Rolls, Damasio, Craig) and against "logical positivism" in animal research. Read the argumentative passages as position-taking, not consensus.
  • Heavily self-cited, and the core claims trace back to Affective Neuroscience (1998), which is not held.
  • No dogs. The species base is rats, with some primate and human ESB work. Every use of this model for a dog-derived robot inherits the same functional-analogy burden as the dog model itself.
  • Contested. Nothing in the corpus offers the opposing view first-hand; the wiki's picture of affective neuroscience is currently Panksepp's own. #needs-source
