Candidate base model #1 — and, on the evidence now in the wiki, the one the research programme this thesis belongs to has already chosen.

The model, as reported in the corpus

Core affects are associated with distinct subcortical regions belonging to different brain circuits, differentiable pharmacologically, neurologically and behaviourally. The circuits named in szabo-2011-emotional-engine:

Circuit Function
SEEKING expectation
FEAR self-defence
RAGE anger
LUST sexuality
CARE parental attachment
PANIC grief, sadness, separation
PLAY joy, happiness

These systems are activated by sensory input; autobiographical memory and higher cortical functions also act on them; and propulsive or inhibitory interactions between circuits are possible.

Provenance — now first-hand

Both primaries are in the corpus as of 2026-07-28: panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness (Consciousness and Cognition 14(1), reference [8]) and panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits (Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev. 35(9)). The circuit list is confirmed. Everything below is now sourced directly rather than via szabo-2011-emotional-engine.

What the primaries add

A terminological hierarchy. Emotion is the umbrella term covering affective, cognitive, behavioural, expressive and physiological change. Affect is the subjective feeling component. Affects come in three kinds — emotional, homeostatic (hunger, thirst; from interoceptors) and sensory (taste, touch, smell, sound). Only the first is what the seven circuits generate.

Dimensions are acknowledged, not denied. Panksepp names valence, arousal and "surgency or power (how much does a certain feeling fill one's mental life)", then argues the causal structure is circuit-based. He also concedes "there is currently no agreed upon taxonomy of affective states". He is not the pure discrete-theory foil to plutchik-model that the wiki assumed.

A specification for an emotional system. Six/seven defining attributes across the two papers: unconditional sensory access (UCS); instinctual behavioural and autonomic outputs (UCR); modulation of sensory input, i.e. incentive salience; positive feedback that sustains arousal after the precipitating event has passed; modulation by cognitive input; and the power to modify higher cognition.

The fourth attribute is the temporal-dynamics layer

"Emotions outlast the stimuli that activated the systems." That is precisely what szabo-2011-emotional-engine built by hand as peak widths, decline rates and saturation levels — and it now has a primary source rather than being an engineering convenience. See emotion-engine.

Three levels of process. Primary (evolutionary "tools for living"), secondary (unconscious learning and memory), tertiary (higher cognition). Mixing them is Panksepp's central methodological complaint, and it is the basis of the contradiction below.

PLAY builds social competence — "to help young animals to acquire the social knowledge and refinement of subtle social interactions that they will need to thrive". See social-competence, and note the convergence with sutton-barto-2018-rl §17.5 describing intrinsic motivation as "a computational analog of something like play".

The "Law of Affect": "'rewards' and 'punishments' would not work unless they changed the way animals feel affectively." Affect is proposed as the mechanism of reinforcement. Bears on Q15 — it argues for the engine being the reward function (reward-design).

How it was used

szabo-2011-emotional-engine: "We applied Panksepp's model to build our core emotional engine. Thus in our model we use several parallel and equal basic emotional states, that represent the different emotional circuitry of the brain. These emotional states can change independently in response on the external events."

Note what was actually taken and what was left:

  • Taken: the principle of several parallel, independent, equally-ranked basic states; the neurobiological grounding.
  • Not taken: the seven-circuit inventory itself. The implemented states are happiness, despair, fear, anger, surprise — derived from dog ethology, not from Panksepp. Only FEAR and RAGE map cleanly; SEEKING, LUST, CARE and PLAY have no counterpart, and surprise has no Panksepp circuit at all.

This is the single most useful thing the ingest revealed about Q1. The prior work did not choose between Panksepp and an ethological state set — it used Panksepp as architectural justification for a state set derived from dog behaviour. That is a third option the wiki had not considered.

Why it fits

  • Discrete parallel states map cleanly onto behaviour selection and emotion-arbitration.
  • Biologically anchored, consistent with ethorobotics' evolutionary framing.
  • Prior implementation exists to build on and to be measured against.
  • The dynamics attribute above supplies the temporal layer for free.

Correction — inter-circuit inhibition is not a basis for arbitration

This page previously claimed that "inter-circuit inhibition gives a principled basis for arbitration rules". panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits says the opposite: "All primal emotional systems interact with each other in primal inhibitory and excitatory relationships that no one has yet mapped empirically."

The interactions are asserted to exist; their structure is unknown. Any arbitration hierarchy claiming Panksepp's authority is therefore unsupported — which is a second problem for emotion-arbitration, on top of the obsolete triune-brain justification already recorded there.

Why it might not

  • No native account of intensity or blending — szabo-2011-emotional-engine had to add saturation levels, peak widths and decline rates by hand, and plutchik-model offers these natively.
  • No obvious place for the robot's internal state (battery, motors). Though see the note on Q2 in open-questions — SEEKING and FEAR arguably are homeostatic-adjacent, which may be a way in. reward-design offers a different and better-sourced route: internal state as a modulator of what is rewarding, the analogue of hunger modulating food reward.
  • The circuits are about mammalian brains. Justifying them for a wheeled robot requires the same functional-analogy argument as the dog model itself.

Contradiction — the SEEKING–dopamine–TD bridge is rejected by Panksepp

On 2026-07-27 this wiki floated a bridge between SEEKING (dopaminergic) and the TD reward-prediction error (standardly interpreted via dopamine), flagged [unsourced] pending a Panksepp primary. The primary arrived and rejects it.

§7 of panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness is titled "The SEEKING/expectancy/wanting system of the brain: It's not just 'reward prediction error'", and the text says emotional systems are "unlikely that they can be encapsulated by the concept of reinforcement or even more subtle variants such as 'reward prediction errors'". panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits repeats it: restricting the view to RPE is "a fundamental error... mixing levels of analysis", and the system was "long misconceptualized as 'The Brain Reward, Pleasure and/or Reinforcement System'".

Against this, sutton-barto-2018-rl §15.3–15.6 presents the reward prediction error hypothesis as well-evidenced and "remarkably resilient".

They are not symmetrical rivals. Panksepp accepts SEEKING is dopaminergic and does not dispute Schultz's data; he disputes that RPE exhausts the system. Sutton & Barto describe a learning signal and never mention affect. For "what does phasic dopamine compute", the RPE hypothesis is better supported; for "what is SEEKING for", Panksepp's level objection stands unopposed.

The bridge must not appear in the thesis in its original form. See td-learning and panksepp-2005-affective-consciousness.
