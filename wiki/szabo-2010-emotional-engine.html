The first version of the engine that szabo-2011-emotional-engine extends. Read the 2011 paper for the mature model; this one is valuable for two things the successor drops.

What is unique to this version

1. A survey of emotional models in six social robots, scored on criteria the authors chose — short-/medium-/long-term response, probabilistic nature, ethological background:

Only this model claims all five. Detail in prior-robot-platforms.

2. A long-term emotional layer, dropped in the 2011 paper. Long-term emotions are the basic emotional settings each emotion converges to when unstimulated — either predefined static values or dynamically generated from long-term event statistics. Formally, y[k] = C(E·xA[k] + d) with d the long-term offset. This is a temperament / personality mechanism, and it is worth recovering.

3. Explicit probabilistic behaviour. The B matrix is resampled per stimulus, B'A = N(B_M, B_σ), from mean and variance matrices, because dogs vary their response to the same context. A variance attribute (high/medium/low) accompanies arousal and valence in the impact table. The 2011 paper drops the variance column.

Design requirements stated

Explicitly targets integration beyond robots — "mobile phone, car, wash machine, intelligent home". Hence: simple arithmetic, time and amplitude scalability, stability via eigenvalue selection, event-triggered background operation, low resource load. It introduces input and output middleware to map physical device I/O onto the abstract model — a clean separation worth adopting for emotion-engine on robot-platform.

Limitations

Same as the successor: simulation only, hand-labelled events, expert-elicited tables, no sensors, no human subjects, no robot internal state. The authors concede there were no experimental data on dogs to set the time constants, and propose setting them from human usage patterns instead.
