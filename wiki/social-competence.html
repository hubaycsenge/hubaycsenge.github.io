The target property for an ethorobot. Defined in miklosi-2017-ethorobotics as

an individual's ability to generate social skills that conform to the expectations of others and the social rules of the group.

It emerges only when three conditions hold: the individual wants to participate, has the means to participate, and is regarded by others as able to participate.

That third condition is the interesting one for robotics — social competence is partly conferred by the partner, not wholly built into the agent. Which is why:

Partnership is a process, not a state

"A social robot does not automatically become a social partner but achieves this state of affairs if it engages in the appropriate kind of social interactions with its partner. Any type of partnership is not an a priori attribute of the robot but actually an outcome of relevant social interactions." — miklosi-2017-ethorobotics

Both the robot's social skills and the time devoted to interaction by both parties determine whether partnership emerges.

Consequence for validation-study: a single 20-minute session cannot measure social competence in this sense; it can only measure legibility of expression. Every empirical study in the corpus (lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution, gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution) is a short single-session study, so none of them actually tests the framework's central construct. That is a real gap, and a possible contribution.

habitual-vs-goal-directed now offers a candidate mechanism and instrument for that gap — a documented shift from goal-directed to habitual control with repeated experience, detectable by outcome devaluation. Q16 in open-questions.

Why the human model is rejected

Approaches like Fong et al. (2003) enumerate the human social skills a robot should have. miklosi-2017-ethorobotics rejects this on two grounds: there is no robust technical solution for most of them, and the biological foundations of human social interaction are obscured by our social and cultural complexity. The dog's competence is simpler and legible — which is the whole argument for dog-human-interaction-model.

Dog-derived components proposed for robots

From miklosi-2017-ethorobotics:

  • attachment to humans (attachment-sst)
  • simple ways of communicative interaction
  • responsiveness to learning and training — see below
  • being useful in some specific way

"Responsiveness to learning and training" is the in-corpus mandate for RL

This item sat unremarked until the sutton-barto-2018-rl ingest. It is a requirement stated by the framing source of the whole thesis, and no system in the corpus satisfies it — every model is hand-authored and fixed at design time (fuzzy-automaton-fri, szabo-2011-emotional-engine).

It is therefore the strongest justification available for the decision recorded in rl-for-behaviour-learning: a trainable robot is not an imported machine-learning ambition but an unmet ethorobotic requirement. It also picks the method — Skinner's shaping (conditioning) is simultaneously the ethologically native form of training and the book's principal remedy for sparse reward (reward-design).

Two further sources now converge on this. panksepp-2011-emotional-circuits gives the PLAY circuit exactly this function — "to help young animals to acquire the social knowledge and refinement of subtle social interactions that they will need to thrive" — and sutton-barto-2018-rl §17.5 independently describes intrinsic motivation as producing "a computational analog of something like play". And topal-2014-ostensive reduces the target from knowledge transfer to behavioural synchronisation, with a five-item attention specification that is directly buildable. See ostensive-signalling.

The last is easy to overlook and is a hard constraint: in this framework social competence without function is not viable, because the robot's niche is its function. See function-orientedness.

"Responsiveness to training" plausibly means responsiveness to being trained — 2026-07-30

The wiki has read that requirement as sensitivity to a reward stream. The ostension ingest suggests it is stronger than that.

Training is not reward delivery; it is a human addressing an animal, and the ostensive cues are how the animal knows a trial is a trial. okumura-2020-ostension-attention shows the two come apart cleanly: matched attention-grabbing cues produced orienting and no learning, while an ostensive cue produced both. An agent that cannot tell training from ambient events treats every reward as an anonymous sample and discards the trainer's intent at the sensor — while passing any benchmark that only measures whether it oriented.

Read that way, an ostension-blind robot fails this requirement even with a perfect reward function, which makes pedagogical-stance part of social competence rather than an addition to it. Also two of Topál's five attention items — monitor and share a common focus — are ostension-recognition in the ethological register. (This reading is the wiki's; miklosi-2017-ethorobotics does not gloss the phrase.) [unsourced]
