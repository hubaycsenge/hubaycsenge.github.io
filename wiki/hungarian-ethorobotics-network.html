The hubs

Ádám Miklósi (ELTE) — the theoretical centre. Author of Dog Behaviour, Evolution and Cognition (OUP), cited by nearly every paper here. Co-author of miklosi-2017-ethorobotics, which names the field.

Márta Gácsi (ELTE) — appears on almost every paper in the corpus, ethology and engineering alike. The connective tissue of the network and first author of gacsi-2016-emotion-attribution.

Péter Korondi (BME) — the engineering counterpart, co-author on the framing paper and on the Szabó, Kovács, Vincze and Chuo papers. The most likely supervisory link for this thesis given research-proposal's framing.

Szilveszter Kovács (Miskolc) — owns the FRI methods (fuzzy-automaton-fri); Dávid Vincze his long-term collaborator and the author of the most recent work in the corpus (vincze-2021-automatic-coding).

What this means for the thesis

The corpus is not independent

The two RO-MAN 2012 papers from Japan (ichikawa-numakunai-2012-roman) look like external corroboration of the dog-based approach, but Korondi and Gácsi are co-authors on both. No genuinely independent group appears in the corpus. Every empirical result supporting the dog model comes from people who proposed the dog model.

This is not a criticism of the work — it is a young field — but a thesis defending it needs either external replication or an explicit acknowledgement. Chasing critical or independent literature is a priority: see citation-backlog. Note that lakatos-2014-emotion-attribution cites work that "argue[s] with its effectiveness" (ref [35] there) — find that paper.
