A learned fear response for MecanumBot in human–robot interaction

Python reinforcement learning Gym-style environments ROS 2 behaviour trees

Description

Dogs that are startled freeze, back away, or turn to their owner for reassurance — and they stay cautious for a while after the threat is gone. This task builds that behaviour into MecanumBot in an interaction scenario: the robot carries out a simple delivery task while a person moves around it, and a person coming too close or too fast raises an internal fear level that decays slowly afterwards.

A reinforcement-learning agent learns when to freeze, avoid (retreat with Nav2), catch the person's attention, or carry on with the task. A first version of the fear dynamics, a training environment and draft behaviour-tree leaves exist; the work is to connect them to real LiDAR input, bring the learned policy into the robot's behaviour tree, and test it in the MuJoCo digital twin and on the robot.

Background — why we do it

In animals, fear is not a reflex that stops when the stimulus disappears: defensive states build up with threat and fade slowly, and the choice between freezing, fleeing and seeking social support depends on the situation and on learning. A robot with a comparable internal state behaves differently from one with a plain safety rule — a proximity-based slow-down returns to full speed the moment a person steps back, whereas a fear-modulated robot remains cautious. Whether people read this residual caution as an emotion, and whether it makes the robot easier to predict, is an ethorobotics question.

Reinforcement learning is a natural fit because which defensive response pays off depends on the consequences — a retreat that costs the task, a freeze that prevents a collision — rather than on a hand-written rule. Keeping the learned policy inside a behaviour tree keeps the robot's behaviour inspectable and safe, which matters when it runs next to people in an experiment.

Requirements for the finished system

  • A fear model driven by the robot's real /scan data: risk from the minimum range and closing speed of people or obstacles, a spike on near-collision, and a slow, parameterised decay.
  • A Gym-style training environment with the discrete actions freeze, avoid, catch attention and task, and the fear level in the observation.
  • A trained policy compared against a fixed hand-written heuristic on task completion, near-collisions and how long caution persists after the person leaves.
  • A ROS 2 behaviour-tree package in which the policy selects between the four responses, with avoid implemented as a Nav2 retreat and freeze cancelling navigation.
  • Runs of the full stack in the MuJoCo digital twin, then on the real robot in the lab with a person present.
  • Every parameter of the fear dynamics and the reward documented, with its biological motivation — these are experimental design choices, reviewed with an ethologist before any study.
  • Unit tests for the fear dynamics and the reward, and launch instructions for simulation and robot.
